# Wind manipulations: physical route decision versus motion performance

**Registered 2026-10-22 before examining individual flight outcome rows.** Independent wind intervention data; this is reanalysis of a published experiment, NOT new experimental finding or direct evidence for natural foraging fitness.

## Original experimental data
Burnett, Badger & Combes (2022) *PLOS ONE* 17:e0265911, Dryad DOI `10.25338/B8Z34Q`, Dryad version `171277`. Official public dataset ZIP is `https://datadryad.org/api/v2/versions/171277/download`. Original member `Bee_route_choice_in_wind.csv` has exactly 22,602 bytes and Dryad SHA256 `2361dccb523b97f1e5e542fc492d302a5e7c290cdde72eb2dfb2ce021153d6f7`. Data paper: 58 individually identified bees, 548 filmed flights with randomized field-obstacle height among bees and within-bee randomized starting still/wind condition. Wind was physically generated with fans at average 0.54 m/s in open corridors; obstacle lee conditions may be lower. Altitude, speed and lateral excursion are measured outcomes, not choices between biological partners.

## Main causal/structural question
Does randomized wind **change route classification** independently of changes in flight performance, at fixed obstacle height and across repeated flights of one bee? Distinguish (P(A\mid W,H)) from (E[V\mid W,H,A]). Do not claim a null route coefficient establishes equivalence; report intervals and detectable effects. Wind direction (up/down-tunnel) can be confounded with unmeasured tunnel asymmetry but it is recorded and controlled. Wind presence is within-bee, obstacle height randomized across bees; do not treat 548 flights as independent animals.

## Frozen outcomes
1. **Route category**: binary `above=1` if **median flight altitude > obstacle height** (a coarse operational proxy only), within canopy if <=; structural 11mm condition must be removed from main route analysis, since obstacle shorter than ordinary low-level flight. Main use 69/98/127 mm groups, 40mm sensitivity. Confirm both wind conditions and route classes in ≥10 independent bees or mark route NO-GO; do not invent per-flight altitude trajectory.
2. **Motion cost**: median ground speed (m/s) and lateral excursion (mm) retain all heights, with relative wind conditions still/headwind/tailwind determined from *Wind Presence* and flight direction label. The predeclared main effect is within-bee **wind vs still**, averaged within bee and matched by direction where possible. Positive/negative signed changes and descriptive bootstrap intervals over bees.
3. **Decision×performance coupling**: route category×wind or route×ground-speed model only as a descriptive diagnostic, not causal effect of route on speed, because route is a post-wind choice/biomechanical response. Split groups by observed support; don't infer flight path strategy optimality from faster motion alone.

## Original schema and QA
Mandatory columns: `Bee ID`, `Canopy height (mm)`, `Wind Presence`, `Flight Direction Label`, `Median altitude (mm)`, `Altitudinal range (mm)`, `Median ground speed (m/s)`, `Lateral excursion (mm)`. All rows must have valid numeric motion measurements; report missing, duplicates, bee ID, wind balance, obstacle heights before outcomes. Do not claim original full 548 retained if missingness exists. No imputation.

## Statistical contract and interpretation
Primary comparisons are within-bee wind/still contrasts on bee-level wind-cell means. Split head/tail only when >0 within-bee observations of relevant directions and still counterpart; report support and cluster bootstrap of bees (4999 draws). Route wind effect should be estimated with bee-paired binary route propensity differences, not by naive row-level Bernoulli p-values. Spatial option / information accessibility are **not measured** (no alternative flower or learned sensory cue recorded). Main positive claim if any may only refer to *wind's direct biomechanical effects conditional on tunnel geometry*, not ecological interaction routeability.

## Other species connection
Published randomized wind-block/ladybeetle–soybean aphid predator studies (Barton 2014, DOI 10.1890/13-2171.1) establish that biotic-mediated wind effects exist in at least one specific system; this is NOT new discovery of a universal principle. UK PoMS wind×larval diet was lineage-confounded and other-guild additions did not generalize. The high-level novel question for adaptive-gain remains whether wind modifies the **time-dependent accessible decision topology** of partners, requiring experimentally varied cues/biological partners, not merely a flying insect's speed.
