## Ecology and identification

Question A: after controlling locality, plant flowering/resource availability, time, temperature, sun and 15-min observation exposure, does a recorded breeze modify the number and composition of plant-pollinator interactions? This is a within-meadow repeated-observation association, not randomized wind or a physical 1 m/s wind slope. Report ST versus BR comparisons only where both conditions occur with independent dates and matched plot, year and flowering context.

Question B (requires minute-level sequences and sufficient support): does **another visitor taxon or guild appearing in the preceding minute** predict focal visitor occurrence during the NEXT observed minute at the same plot/watch, beyond same-visitor autoregression and the watch's base rate? Does this lagged association differ between ST/BR? Unlike PoMS other-trap synchronous co-capture, the putative cross-taxon cue occurs *earlier*. This is a conditional predictive proxy, not an observed information query, competition, attraction, predation or an individual animal response.

Before modeling:
1. Verify 15 distinct observation minutes and watch identity, within-watch order and missingness. Distinguish repeated visits by the same animal from visitor taxon identities. Retain count-weight `NO_INT` only when it means the documented number of interactions, not number of distinct insects.
2. Aggregate presence/counts by minute and **guild or taxon** without converting unobserved species to absence at a flower not independently observed blooming. If wind varies only at watch level, never infer within-watch wind intervention.
3. Require >=30 independently monitored site-days with each wind class for main wind contrast; >=200 qualifying within-watch consecutive minute pairs, >=30 site-days, and >=20 watches with meaningful different-guild lag variation for the cross-taxon test. Subsets failing gates -> NO-GO; keep valid source QA.
4. Predefine primary other-taxon indicator as *any different taxon visitor recorded previous minute*, contrasted to **same visitor taxon in previous minute**, not selecting favorite taxon-pairs after seeing results. Analyze per-minute target visitor presence plus other-guild presence, with site/site-year and watch-context correction; leave entire meadow/year blocks out when possible. Use a within-watch **circular time shift of whole multivariate minute-event vectors** or comparable randomization that retains daily effort and autocorrelation when generating nulls.
5. Stratify by source-coded taxa only where resolution allows. Include detection, weather, floral resource and observation changes, rare types and site-dependence in sensitivity checks.

## Novelty ceiling and existing negative evidence

Previous 92-site UK PoMS pan-trap study showed strong same-guild synchronous near-trap prediction but no stable extra predictive benefit from unrelated coarse parasitoid wasp/spider guilds or wind×heteroguild terms. Earlier UK PoMS `wind×larval feeding class` RR 1.62 was almost completely genus/lineage-confounded. Crall tropical orchid bees show wind-related scent-bait arrival changes but species composition is metric-sensitive. This 11-year true observed plant–visitor minute record provides an **independent interaction and time-order data set**; it cannot independently identify parasitoid/prey links because no such activity is directly recorded.

Conclude an empirical indirect/species interaction only with measured pairwise biotic links or intervention. Otherwise report a temporally ordered prediction, with no adaptive cue-routeability or fitness inference.
