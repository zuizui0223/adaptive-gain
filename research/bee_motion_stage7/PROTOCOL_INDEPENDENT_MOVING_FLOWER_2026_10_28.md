# Stage 7 — independent moving-flower bumblebee trajectories, source-first

**Protocol recorded 2026-10-28 before analyzing independent source kinematics. Exploratory as an extension of the Stage 4–6 geometric direction findings.** The focus is a different biological process and observational unit, not a replication of the source Stage 6 ten-flower route network.

## Source, original experiment, external independence
de Vries et al. (2025), *Follow the flower: Approach-flight behaviour of bumblebees landing on a moving target*, *Journal of Experimental Biology*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.249380. Original publicly archived Dryad dataset DOI **https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.qbzkh18tj**, published 2025-03-19.

Publisher describes **728 filtered successful landing trajectories from two bumblebee hives** with flower movement at frequencies **0, 0.03, 0.24, 0.53 and 0.65 Hz**, amplitude approximately **5 cm**, under robotic sinusoidal motion. Original paper also recorded *unsuccessful* landings, but the published kinematic folder contains only the 728 filtered successful events; **do not estimate landing success probability, treatment-caused survival, or direction-dependent fitness from this success-conditioned dataset.** The original publication already established that landing speed, heading and relative velocity depend on flower motion; ordinary reproduction of those findings is not a new discovery.

The dataset contains a `.7z` original archive with 30 bee and 18 flower kinematic CSV files, 300 time steps per column and 728 original landing trajectories; separate trajectory-level `flower_frequency.csv`, `hive_number.csv`, `day_number.csv`, and `time_s.csv`. Original README and source publication report 2D x/y positions, velocities and accelerations; **verify which axis is flower movement against the original README and source columns before computing a response.** The description calls the flower "vertically" moving in one place while original paper says "sideways"; use the original flower kinematics and Cartesian axis conventions, not the ambiguous prose label.

### Prespecified science direction; metric details to freeze after schema audit but before kinematic endpoint inspection
The substantive question is **whether motion-induced flower displacement is compensated by temporal flower–bee kinematic coupling, and whether that coupling changes with oscillation frequency** after controlling for the changing instantaneous target position. Compare *real observed trajectory kinematics* rather than inferring headings from a sequence of fixed flower IDs. Candidate diagnostic is a paired per-trajectory projection of **bee lateral velocity onto flower lateral velocity** over a pre-touchdown time interval, and a temporally lagged target-motion predictor. Physical lag and trajectory/smoothing definitions will be frozen using original time-vector support; never select on observed test-set accuracy or subgroup significance.

Primary forecasting guard: any fitted motion-response weight is to be estimated on **one original hive only**, evaluated without refitting on the other hive, with reverse holdout as sensitivity. Flower-frequency groups and source day must remain explicit. A static position-only predictor is the minimum fair comparator; model improvement must be evaluated at the *trajectory*, not frame, level. Source paper's lack of identity tags may limit cross-hive generality, and a sample with 22 successes at 0.65 Hz imposes a sharp precision ceiling.

No inference of wind direction, gust turbulence, self-versus-other species interactions, sensory-cue access order, Markov reward policy, evolutionary adaptability or reproductive fitness; this experiment manipulates **flower motion**, not ambient wind, interspecific competition or ten-flower spatial destination options. Treat this as independent *motion-scale* evidence and keep the Stage4–6 and frozen V5/V6 manuscripts untouched.
