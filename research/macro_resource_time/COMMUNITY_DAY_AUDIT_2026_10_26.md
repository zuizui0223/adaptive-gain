# Post-hoc Stage 2: community-day conditional partner/time audit (2026-10-26)

**Status:** exploratory audit implementation; **NOT** a completed new natural-data result until the exact original-data workflow prints \`COMMUNITY_DAY_ANALYSIS_COMPLETE\` and its receipts are inspected. The original 2026-10-19 protocol, 457-panel eligibility thresholds and frozen paper surfaces are unchanged.

## Why this is different from the first temporal null

The original test preserves each focal visitor's date and plant totals over the full season. Consequently, large excess I(plant;date | visitor) can be generated by community-wide changes in which plants are observed at all. In particular, it is not proof of a visitor-specific choice process.

For each compiled study × sSite × year group, retain **all** positive day-grain visitors, including those not eligible as focal panels. Independently permute the observed **plant labels** within each date while leaving visitor and date labels fixed. This conserves the observed margins

- date × plant: pooled recorded visits to every plant on each date;
- date × visitor: recorded visits of every visitor taxon on each date.

It does **not** conserve visitor × plant season totals. Thus visitor specialization, date-varying behavior, source-level recording clusters, taxon phenology and interactions between these effects may still change the result. The conditional null's meaning is *exchangeability of observed plant labels across recorded visitor events within one site-day*, **not** equal access to blooming plants.

Compute each initially eligible focal visitor's observed I(plant;date) minus 199 Monte Carlo conditional-null draws. Report one-sided Monte Carlo tails with the standard plus-one convention, median excess and explicit testability (a focal visitor co-occurs with other visitor taxa and at least two plants on a sampled day). Do **not** interpret degenerate p=1 as biological evidence of no visitor-specific time effect.

## Visit versus source-row observational units

The original compilation contains some rows with freq>1, plus many repeated full rows. One event-level shuffle expands each row according to its reported visit frequency. A second, preregistered-to-this-stage **sensitivity** treats each original positive source row as one unit without dropping duplicate rows. Both retain their respective day-plant and day-visitor margins exactly. They address dependence on multiplicity semantics but are **not** an observer-effort correction and must not be sold as independent replicate animals.

Strong signals appearing only under expanded visits may be dominated by within-row count clustering or sampling protocol. Neither result by itself identifies individual behavior, flowering availability, detection-adjusted link changes, fitness, cue order or routing values CA/CF.

## What would be a new ecological finding?

Positive residuals reproducible across at least several independent original studies and across both observational-unit policies would support **visitor-taxon-specific time structure beyond the pooled recorded site-day plant composition**. This would still fall short of identifying a behavioral preference change because the data contain neither simultaneously available flowers nor non-detections or per-plant observation exposure.

If the residual disappears, the original positive finding still holds, but shared seasonal plant composition becomes a parsimonious explanation. If only the visit-unit result survives, investigate the survey/count unit and overdispersion before publishing a biological effect.

An independent zero-inclusive focal-plant dataset with flowering abundance and comparable exposure remains the critical third-stage ecological test; a randomized sequence-of-cues study remains necessary for causal routeability.
