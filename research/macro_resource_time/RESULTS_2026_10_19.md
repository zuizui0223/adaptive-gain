There are **114,965 positive day-grain rows**, representing **186,950 recorded daily-grain visits**, from 23 source study labels. From **14,607** positive daily panels, **457** (3.13%) qualify, covering **102,891** recorded visits (55.04% of daily-grain visit counts) across **20 source labels**. ## Main scale-resolved estimands

For each qualifying taxon-site-season table (day × plant), compute

\[
I(\mathrm{plant};\mathrm{date}) = H(\mathrm{plant}) - H(\mathrm{plant}\mid\mathrm{date}).
\]

Define effective pooled diversity `Dpool=exp(H(plant))`, within-day diversity `Dwithin=exp(H(plant|date))`, and observed ratio `Dpool/Dwithin=exp(I)`. Across 457 eligible panels, **median Dpool=4.0402**, **median Dwithin=1.8268**, and **median within-panel ratio=2.2092**. A ratio >=1 is algebraically necessary; it is NOT the inferential result.

Use 199 randomizations per panel, preserving observed totals by date and by plant. Define excess `I_observed - mean(I_permuted)`, with one-sided permutation tail `p=(1+#perm>=obs)/(200)`. This is a conditional visit-label exchangeability null, not an observer-effort/detection correction.

| Permutation resolution | Testable panels | p<=.05 / testable | Median excess I across all 457 panels (nats) | Median permutable-visit fraction across all panels |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Whole season | 457 | **440/457 = 96.28%** | 0.458145 | 1.0000 |
| 28-day blocks | 450 | **373/450 = 82.89%** | 0.196182 | 1.0000 |
| 14-day blocks | 441 | **327/441 = 74.15%** | 0.091303 | 0.9333 |
| 7-day blocks | 373 | **231/373 = 61.93%** | 0.032071 | 0.6821 |

The **median per-panel excess factor** under whole-season fixed margins is `exp(0.458145)=about 1.58`; this is the effect beyond the purely mathematical >=1 diversity ratio and the finite sample's fixed plant/date marginals. The analogous 28-day median excess factor is about 1.217. Local blocked nulls keep broad seasonal differences between blocks, thus their residuals need not equal whole-season excess. In particular, a block lacking at least two visited dates *and* two visited plants cannot shuffle: a p=1 for such a panel is **non-testability, not a detected biological zero**.

Study-label summaries: mean whole-season excess I positive in **20/20** eligible source labels; an equal-label average excess is **0.390316** nats. Positive 7-day mean excess occurs in 19/20 source labels; this includes labels with very few testable 7-day panels. Source labels, especially multiple WinfreeYY variants, are **not established independent original studies**. These are descriptive cross-source signs, not independent-study meta-analytic p-values.

## Ecological result (allowed inference)

Within well-sampled visitor-taxon/site/season panels, observed plant-partner composition is often **temporally non-exchangeable even after plant-total and date-total visitation counts are held fixed**. A visitor's season-long pooled apparent partner breadth frequently does not represent its partner composition on a sampled day. Time structure is seen both across weeks and (among testable panels) within 7-day blocks.

This is an original data-derived, positive pattern; it is not equivalent to the trivial identity `I>=0`. It is compatible with phenological turnover or temporally varying access to partners. It does **not** show the animal's perceived simultaneous option set or prove that time segregation itself caused an adaptive information-routing advantage.

## Main outstanding identification problems

1. Positive-only visit data lack focal flower-survey denominators, visits per plant exposure, plant flowering abundance and non-detection records. Fixed visit margins do **not** control changing focal-plant survey allocation, detection or availability.
2. The visitor identifier `higher` can be a species/morphospecies and does NOT identify one animal. Panel-level temporal structure may reflect population/colony turnover, not within-individual decisions.
3. Compiled source dates can be artificially shifted, and some source sites are pooled. Do not join actual-day climate or interpret date differences as behavioral deadlines.
4. Panel tests overlap within site/study; p<=0.05 fractions are unadjusted descriptive summaries, not a pooled hypothesis test. Study-origin independence needs checking against the original-studies workbook.
5. The eligibility cut captures 3.13% of daily panels and over half the recorded daily-grain visits; do not extrapolate frequency to rare, undersampled visitors.
