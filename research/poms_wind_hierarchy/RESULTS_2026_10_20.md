## 1. Wind and simultaneous pan-trap catches
Original UK PoMS pan-trap station source DOI `10.5285/5841bfae-471b-498c-af89-c30e5140d6c5`. Undisturbed 4–8h trap station records, same-day within-site contrasts: 5,506 stations, 1,139 site-days, 92 sites. Condition out site/date, control station exposure, relative position, vegetation height, sunlight, temperature; site-cluster CIs. Exposure is averaged ordinal deploy/retrieve Beaufort score, **not measured m/s**.

## 2. Wind-dependent apparent links among otherwise unrelated groups
Fit 12 separate guild capture models, derive same-day/site station residuals, compare calm (wind<=1.5) versus brisk (>=2.5) among 66 pairs with whole-site bootstrap and BH.

| Co-capture pair | low-wind r | brisk-wind r | difference (brisk-low) | CI95 | BH q across 66 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Other flies – butterflies | -0.032 | 0.083 | 0.115 | [0.050,0.180] | 0.083 |
| Solitary wasps – unresolved other insects | 0.069 | -0.031 | -0.100 | [-0.166,-0.035] | 0.083 |
| Bees – parasitoid wasps | 0.017 | 0.093 | 0.076 | [0.016,0.135] | 0.347 |
| Hoverflies – other beetles | -0.007 | 0.045 | 0.052 | [-0.009,0.113] | 0.591 |

**None of the 66 wind-modulated residual links survives q<0.05.** These are small group-level co-capture correlations; different taxa may share traps, shelter and flowering plants, rather than influence each other. Actual species-level predation/parasitoidism/competition was NOT measured.

## 3. Does nonfocal bycatch help at completely held-out sites?
Exact same-date FIT (10 minutes) matched to pan traps (4–8h), **1,489 FIT surveys, 762 site-days, 89 sites**; five whole-site holdout folds. Baseline includes focal flower, floral units, season, weather, two ordinal wind measurements, nearby recorded flowering-taxon richness and the focal guild's own independent pan catch. Competitors: +nonfocal captures; +nonfocal captures × FIT wind composite. Site-equal Poisson NLL improvement, **positive means predictive gain, not biological benefit**.

| Outcome, penalty alpha=.25 | +nonfocal taxa versus baseline | +nonfocal and wind interaction versus baseline |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Hoverfly FIT counts | **-0.620%** | +0.399% |
| Bee FIT counts | **-0.763%** | -0.732% |

For hoverflies the full versus baseline site-score bootstrap CI of absolute loss gain is [-.0488,+.0635], crossing zero. Other penalties alpha=.025 and 2.5 yield sign instability of the nonfocal-only effect (hoverfly -1.062% vs +2.024%). Therefore **no robust extra predictive value of apparently unrelated taxa is established**; do not describe a wind-dependent biological interaction network as discovered.

## 4. Does wind account for the seasonal hoverfly visit increase?
Previous 91 same-site/year/flower-type paired early/late comparisons shrink to 90 after wind-completeness restriction, still **47 sites**. Re-estimate every comparator on this identical set. Whole-site bootstrap with model refitting (1999 draws):
- M3 with plants, pan own-guild catch, recorded floral richness/coverage and temperature, no wind: **+1.197 visits/10min** [+.375,+2.065].
- M4 plus FIT wind shift: **+1.226** [+.387,+2.099].
- M5 plus both FIT leaf wind and trap Beaufort shifts: **+1.209** [+.374,+2.024].
- M5 wind coefficient intervals cover zero, so no measured wind mediation identified. Bees M5 -0.201 [-1.333,+1.005] not resolved.

## Scientific claim ceiling and proposed falsifiable mechanism
Environment → wind / flower movement / shelter → differentiated guild activity and trap catch; environmental opportunity and other taxa may change access to a focal flower, but neither an interspecific causal edge nor the adaptive-gain conditional cue-choice mechanism follows from the observed group co-capture. To identify actual hierarchical biotic interactions: species-resolved simultaneously recorded flower visits, prey/host records for parasitoids/spiders, independently measured alternative floral resource availability, calibrated wind at flower height, adequate detection/effort data, or randomized windbreak × shared resource treatments.
