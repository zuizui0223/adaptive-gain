# Stage30 — floral visual signals as decision-routing information: preregistration-ready scoping protocol

**2026-11-15 — SOURCE IDENTIFICATION AND STUDY DESIGN ONLY. No novel meta-analytic effects, no new field observations, no observed adaptive information fitness benefit.** This is an ecological translation and scoping evidence map stacked after Stage29; the previous bee trial findings and mathematical Stage22–24 theorems remain frozen. Main and existing manuscript claims are untouched.

## Primary scientific question

**At which decision stage do nectar guides, floral patterns, UV markings, and age-/pollination-related colour changes alter the routes taken by pollinators, and under which sensory, learning, reward and display conditions do those reroutings translate into plant reproductive success?**

This is **NOT** equivalent to asking whether flower colour is generally attractive, whether UV-rich flowers are preferred, or whether signal-reward correlation is positive. Existing theory has already formalized floral cue honesty, reliability, information content and honest colour-change evolution. Our candidate distinction is **sequential policy value conditional on alternative actions, perceptual reliability, receiver memory, motor/time costs and conflicts between plant and pollinator objectives**.

### Pre-existing work we must not rebrand as new

- Evolutionary ecology of nectar (2018): meta-analysis of floral signals vs nectar volume/sugar; correlations generally weak and positive; many signals are size, not necessarily colour or a route guide. DOI: 10.1093/aob/mcy132.
- Caruso et al. (2019), DOI: 10.1111/evo.13639, 36 articles/755 records for the analyzable standardized directional-selection-gradients subset; manipulative pollinator selection generally stronger on efficiency traits than flower-level attraction. Original Dryad DOI: 10.5061/dryad.2v8c5g0. These are gradients, not guide-specific phase effects.
- Solanaceae floral traits systematic review and meta-analysis (2026), DOI: 10.1093/botlinnean/boag036: 128 studies/249 plant taxa, with pollinator groups associated with colour, spots, symmetry, rewards. The study's taxon-pollinator GLM associations are not a pooled causal guide-removal contrast.
- Ruxton and Schaefer (2016) 10.1016/j.pbi.2016.06.021 and Suzuki/Ohashi/Makino (2011) 10.18960/seitai.61.3_259 reviewed floral colour changes and ecological conditions; they are not equivalent to a quantitative effect-size synthesis for each sequential choice.
- Evolution of honest reward signal in flowers (2021) 10.1098/rspb.2020.2848: an existing evolutionary model finds mixed learner abilities important in maintaining honest colour-change signalling.
- Honesty, reliability, and information content of floral signals (2023) 10.1016/j.isci.2023.107093: already provides formal cue/signal and signal-reward concepts. The candidate novelty here is neither the word "information" nor the presence of a signal-reward correlation.

**Literature search is an initial targeted scoping search, not a complete PRISMA search. The absence of a unified guide-specific stage-value meta-analysis is NOT proven; recheck a registered database query before claiming novelty.**

## Causal structure and nontrivial counterpredictions

Pollinator sensory perception, encounter probability and actual signal usage are separate variables. Let G denote a floral marking and pi a pollinator policy conditional on sensory observations.

- Pollinator value for a particular ecological decision: V_P(G)=max_pi E[nectar/pollen reward - energy/time/motor cost - unrewarded visits | observations of G, local environment, learning history]. A free observable pattern may be ignored at zero acquisition cost, yet still alter movement/navigation costs and error rates. **Do not insert Stage23's exact cost thresholds 47/350 or 27/100 as empirical floral cutoffs.**
- Plant value: V_F(G)=expected outcross pollen transfer, seeds and siring success minus signal construction costs, geitonogamy, flower robbery and other antagonistic responses. V_P and V_F need not have equal signs.
- Distinguish spatial information about the *position* of the corolla entrance, I(G; entrance location | floral geometry), from information about *whether a nectar reward exists*, I(G; reward presence | age, population, background). A static radial nectar guide can carry spatial guidance even when it carries no information about current nectar content.
- A flower with a guide can show **zero long-range approach gain** but strong **proboscis insertion / pollen-transfer gain**; this is documented by Hansen et al. (2012) 10.1098/rspb.2011.1349.
- Flower patterns can reduce total handling time by up to ~30% through approach, landing and departure with no faster actual post-landing nectar discovery; see 10.1111/1365-2435.14262 (2023).
- Guides can change legitimate visits versus nectar robbing; see 10.1371/journal.pone.0055914 (2013).
- Learned patterns can cause visitation to persist after reward removal; benefit may favor plants rather than animals; see 10.1111/j.1365-2435.2011.01885.x (2011).
- Floral colour-change effects can change sign across old-flower retention, display size and within-plant flowering schedule; see PMID 17674052 (2007).
- A multiple-receiver community can create antagonistic or nonadditive selection on flower colour, including secondary pollinators; see PubMed 41667093 (2026). Avoid reducing the response to a single group mean.

### Falsifiable hypotheses for a synthesis

H1 *Stage partition*: compared with uniform floral displays, true guide manipulation yields larger positive contrasts for landing accuracy and legitimate nectar/pollen access than for long-range approach probability. A null or negative approach effect DOES NOT refute a handling/fitness benefit.

H2 *Conditional route value*: guide effects on effective pollen export/receipt depend on perceptual contrast, floral geometry, motor/search difficulty, bee experience and floral reward state. Higher pigment saturation or more visual information does NOT have a universal positive monotone benefit.

H3 *Temporal signal value*: post-pollination or age-related colour change changes choice among old/young flowers as a function of retain-old-display size, flowering synchrony, reward depletion and pollinator learning. Visits per stay and geitonogamy may decrease while plant-level attraction is maintained; which plant fitness component benefits remains an independent test.

H4 *Conflict and cheating*: when a plant's expected pollen transfer and pollinator expected net reward are opposed, a nectar guide can increase legitimate pollen-transfer visits yet lower animal reward return. Signal honesty and spatial guidance must be coded independently.

H5 *Community / environmental interactions*: measured decision-stage benefits differ by pollinator vision/specialization and competitive context; wind and other motor disturbances may affect route costs. Island bee scarcity or artificial night light effects are hypotheses requiring actual independent data, NOT currently positive empirical claims.

## Two linked evidence products, not an indiscriminate pooled effect

### A. Primary experimental phase meta-analysis

PROTOCOL BEFORE QUANTITATIVE EXTRACTION:
1. Search Web of Science, Scopus, Crossref/OpenAlex and relevant open indexes, document query date/strings and dedup DOI; supplement forward/backward citations. Search examples: ("nectar guide" OR "floral pattern" OR "ultraviolet bullseye" OR "flower colour change" OR "floral color change") AND (experiment OR manipulation OR removal OR masking OR "artificial flower" OR pollinator) AND (behavio* OR fitness OR handling OR landing OR pollen). Use pre-registered inclusion rules, screen at title/abstract and full text, document exclusion reasons and non-duplicate independent experiments.
2. Target a direct contrast that manipulates floral signal while controlling flower size, reward state, geometry, odour, painted-surface sham effects and observer visibility. Record lab artificial-vs-natural context, subject identity and study grouping. Observational trait associations belong in a distinct evidence stratum.
3. Build one original-source ledger at **study × experiment × taxon × pollinator guild × treatment × decision stage**, not "one paper = one independent effect", and link original data DOI/URL, manipulation and sample size.
4. FIVE non-interchangeable primary endpoints:
   - distance/initial detection and approach probability or counts;
   - flower landing, body orientation and flower handling time;
   - reward discovery and rewarded/unrewarded legitimate vs nectar-robbing path;
   - pollen removal, legitimate stigma contact, outcross pollen deposition;
   - male siring, fruit set, seeds per flower/plant (true plant fitness).
   Record bee energetic/net reward and geitonogamy separately, never silently as plant fitness.
5. **Effect estimands and variance**: log response ratio (for strictly positive count/time outcomes with appropriate sampling), log odds ratio for binary land/insertion/legitimacy probabilities with treatment denominators, and standardized selection gradients if supplied with SE. Keep these endpoint families separate or fit a properly multivariate joint model with their own units and link functions; **never** average an odds ratio, time ratio and seed-set regression slope as one "floral information effect".
6. Fit hierarchical/multivariate meta-regressions with sampling variances, nonindependent effects within article/experiment, plant and animal phylogenetic structure where data support it, laboratory-vs-field design and moderator preregistration. Precheck missing variances; use sensitivity to unreported covariances, selection bias/publication bias and sham-masking confounds. Do not declare a pooled estimate until a systematic search and source-verified extraction are complete. For sparsely studied stages, publish an evidence map rather than fabricated meta-analysis.
7. Functional distinction: stimuli can be UV-only, visible-only, colour+UV, floral symmetry or temporal age-signal. Code whether rewarded flowers were actually rewarding at observation time. Floral colour changes associated with senescence, temperature or pollination require distinct coding and cannot be assumed to be reliable nectar signals.
8. Report conditional sign and interactions, not just average >0. Prespecified inferential target is a **phase-by-signal-type interaction** between attraction and orientation/handling and, independently, realized plant fitness.

### B. Independent macroecological flower-signal/reward database

Rely on externally accessible original plant records rather than manually extracting only a handful of published effect estimates. Stage candidate sources:

- **2014 wild UV bullseye manipulation source**: Koski and Ashman experiment, Dryad DOI 10.5061/dryad.bm558 (paired dataset FEKoskiSF2.xlsx), for an auditable initial example of choice/flight/UV pattern stages, not a global sample.
- **2019 selection dataset**: Caruso et al. Dryad 10.5061/dryad.2v8c5g0, source records and SEs; keep this meta database as comparison and do not relabel its family-wide trait gradients as independent guide manipulations.
- **2024 reflectance raw data**: DOI 10.1016/j.dib.2024.110512, 73 species and spectrophotometry. Check permission and spectral coverage.
- **2025 island–mainland bee visitation + floral reflectance**: Dryad DOI 10.5061/dryad.612jm64gq with reflectance data for 98 plant species and bee visitation to bee-visible colour categories. This provides a directly relevant independent macro scale **association** test, not proof of selection on guides.
- **2026 Neotropical UV reflectance dataset**, PMC12710832 (245 species across 71 families including 204 previously unreported spectra): source-verify supplementary files and taxon coverage before inclusion.
- Verified raw photos (separate image IDs), calibrated UV + visible spectra per floral organ, observation date, age / pollination state, plant population, background irradiance, and reward volume/sugar estimates where actually measured. GBIF specimen or photograph occurrence by itself does NOT identify nectar rewards, species-level UV markings or within-flower time changes.
- Resolve GBIF and taxonomic synonyms to plant taxa; harmonize *within-organism* signal-reward alignment, photo detection probabilities and environmental sampling effort. A colour category pooled across taxa is not a flower-specific guide/reward reliability estimate.

Possible macro synthesis: plant phylogenetic model of spatial-guide type and age-related change vs pollinator visual guild/availability, geometry and wind/disturbance, with observed state measurement model. Link stage-specific behavioral effect predictions ONLY in groups with independent experimental support. Macro field association estimates never substitute for an intervention-based adaptive fitness claim.

## Decision gates / STOP criteria

- **Do not infer UV reflectance from ordinary RGB images.** Multiband/spectral calibration or true UV source is mandatory for UV guide presence/strength; RGB-only records are "UV unknown", not "UV absent".
- **Do not infer floral colour change** by comparing random flowers of the same species without age/pollination provenance; ontogenetic vs interindividual colour polymorphism are distinct evolutionary objects.
- **Do not equate pollinator approach with success, legitimate visit with outcross fertilization, or pollination with seed set** without separate observations and plausible causal linkage.
- **Do not call the Stage23 cost optimum or Stage24 0.01008 bound a floral result**; they remain mathematical evidence for a different finite policy topology.
- **Do not claim "first universal meta-analysis"** without a dated search and existing-framework comparison; pre-existing 2018/2019/2026 syntheses and 2021/2023 formal theory prevent broad novelty assertions.
- **Do not manufacture a study-level effect size or variance** from abstract-only qualitative descriptions or combine nonexchangeable outcomes.
- Draft branch and seed evidence map are a **scoping artifact**, not a completed PRISMA systematic review or statistical meta-analysis.
