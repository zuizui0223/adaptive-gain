# Abstract v1

A uniquely optimal genotype need not generate the largest—or the majority—stationary phenotype class when genotype–phenotype representation makes slightly inferior states much more numerous. Although competition between selection and genotype multiplicity is established, its exact consequences depend on the representation being selected over. We analyze a finite routing genotype-policy space `X={0,...,q}^k` in which gain is set by the weakest branch, `g(x)=min_i x_i`. The number of genotypes at gain `r` is exactly `D_r=(q-r+1)^k-(q-r)^k`. Writing `A_s=(s+1)^k-s^k` for the layer `s` steps below full gain, we prove `A_s<=(2^k-1)^s`, with equality only at `s=1` for `k>=2`. Under a symmetric stationary tilt `theta`, this yields an exact aggregate-mode transition: below `T_k=2^k-1` the full-gain layer is nonmodal, at equality exactly the full and adjacent layers tie, and above it full gain is uniquely modal. We then separate this from stationary majority. The full-gain mass is `P_full=[1+sum_{s=1}^q A_s theta^{-s}]^{-1}` and crosses one half at a unique `theta_1/2`; for `q>=2`, `T_k<theta_1/2<2T_k`. Under standard Moran origin-fixation weighting, `theta=a^(N-1)`. In the canonical routing construction `k=q+1` with `a=2`, unique aggregate modality occurs at `N=q+2`, whereas stationary majority occurs one population-size step later, at `N=q+3` for every `q>=2`. Exact representation and reversible-mutation counterexamples show that neither threshold is universal: changing the genotype-policy encoding or neutral mutation measure can reverse stationary conclusions while preserving phenotype-level fitness. Thus finite routing architecture converts representation-induced multiplicity into exact, assumption-explicit occupancy thresholds.
