# Abstract v0

A uniquely optimal genotype need not belong to the most abundant phenotype class when genotype–phenotype representation makes slightly inferior phenotypes much more numerous. Although competition between selection and genotype multiplicity is well established, its exact consequences depend on the representation being selected over. We analyze a finite routing genotype-policy space `X={0,...,q}^k` in which routing gain is set by the weakest branch, `g(x)=min_i x_i`. The number of genotypes at gain `r` is exactly `D_r=(q-r+1)^k-(q-r)^k`. Writing `A_s=(s+1)^k-s^k` for the layer `s` steps below full gain, we prove the global bound `A_s<=(2^k-1)^s`, with equality only at `s=1` for `k>=2`. Under a symmetric stationary tilt `theta` per gain step, aggregate layer weight is `D_r theta^r`; the bound therefore yields a sharp transition. If `theta<2^k-1`, the full-gain layer is nonmodal; at equality, exactly the full and adjacent layers tie; above the threshold, the full-gain layer is uniquely modal. Standard Moran origin-fixation weighting gives the corollary `theta=a^(N-1)`, so the transition occurs when `a^(N-1)` crosses `2^k-1`. The threshold concerns aggregate gain-layer mass, not the probability ranking of individual genotypes. Finally, exact representation and mutation-bias counterexamples show that the threshold is conditional: changing the genotype-policy encoding or reversible neutral mutation measure can reverse the stationary conclusion while preserving phenotype-level fitness. Thus finite routing architecture converts representation-induced multiplicity into an exact, assumption-explicit selection threshold.
