Dear Editors of Theoretical Population Biology,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Exact occupancy thresholds in finite routing representations,”** for publication in *Theoretical Population Biology*.

The paper asks a finite-population-genetic question: when a high-fitness phenotype is represented by fewer genotypes than lower-fitness alternatives, when does selection make that phenotype the largest aggregate stationary class, and when does it carry most stationary probability? Selection–multiplicity tradeoffs, reversible weak-mutation stationary weighting, weakest-link fitness, and representation-dependent evolutionary thresholds are established ideas. Our contribution is deliberately narrower. For an explicit finite branch-product genotype-policy representation, we derive the exact gain-layer multiplicities and show that one adjacent-layer obstruction controls all lower layers. Combined with the standard Moran origin-fixation stationary tilt, this yields two distinct occupancy transitions: an exact aggregate-mode threshold at `2^k-1` and a stronger stationary-majority threshold lying strictly between `2^k-1` and `2(2^k-1)` for `q>=2`.

The distinction is population-genetically substantive. In the canonical family with a twofold fitness step, full gain becomes the unique aggregate mode at population size `N=q+2` but does not become a stationary majority until `N=q+3`. We also give representation and reversible mutation-measure counterexamples showing why these thresholds are conditional on the declared genotype–phenotype representation rather than universal consequences of phenotype-level fitness values.

We believe the manuscript fits *Theoretical Population Biology* because it uses an established finite-population process to derive an exact, assumption-explicit consequence of genotype–phenotype structure for stationary population occupancy. The manuscript is a compact theory paper and does not claim a new mutation–selection framework or a universal biological threshold.

The repository contains exact-arithmetic implementations, regression tests, and deterministic generation of the three manuscript figures. No empirical dataset is analyzed in this paper.
