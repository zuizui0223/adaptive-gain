# Cover letter — Theoretical Population Biology

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Exact occupancy thresholds in finite routing representations,”** for publication in *Theoretical Population Biology*.

The paper asks a finite-population-genetic question: when phenotype-level selection favors a unique optimum, how strongly can an explicit genotype–phenotype representation delay that optimum from becoming the largest stationary phenotype class, or from containing most stationary probability?

For a finite branch-product routing representation, we derive the exact gain-layer multiplicities and show that the adjacent suboptimal layer controls every lower layer. Combined with the established reversible weak-mutation origin–fixation stationary law, this gives two distinct occupancy transitions. The full-gain class becomes the unique aggregate mode at the exact tilt `2^k-1`, whereas stationary majority occurs only at a larger threshold lying strictly between `2^k-1` and `2(2^k-1)` for nontrivial depth. In the canonical Moran example with a twofold fitness step, these become adjacent but distinct population-size thresholds.

We have deliberately limited the novelty claim. Selection–multiplicity tradeoffs, weakest-link fitness, reversible origin–fixation weighting, and representation-dependent evolutionary thresholds all have substantial prior literature. The contribution here is the exact composition for the declared finite routing representation, together with explicit representation and mutation-measure counterexamples that delimit where the result can be exported.

We believe the manuscript fits *Theoretical Population Biology* because its main contribution is a compact population-genetic theory result: a specified genotype–phenotype representation is translated through a standard finite-population stationary process into exact predictions for population occupancy. The result is fully reproducible; the repository contains exact-arithmetic tests, the theorem implementation, and deterministic generation of all three figures.
