# Cover letter draft — Theoretical Population Biology

Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Exact occupancy thresholds in finite routing representations,”** for publication in *Theoretical Population Biology*.

The paper asks a population-genetic question that arises when selection acts on a phenotype represented by many genotypes: when does the fittest phenotype become the largest stationary class, and when does it carry most of the stationary probability? We answer these questions exactly for a finite routing genotype-policy representation under a standard weak-mutation origin-fixation stationary law.

The representation yields exact gain-layer multiplicities and an all-layer bound controlled by the adjacent suboptimal layer. This gives a sharp aggregate-mode threshold, `theta=2^k-1`, together with a distinct stationary-majority threshold satisfying `T_k < theta_1/2 < 2T_k` for `q>=2`. In the canonical Moran family with `k=q+1` and a twofold fitness step, the two transitions occur at adjacent population sizes: `N=q+2` for unique aggregate modality and `N=q+3` for stationary majority.

The contribution is deliberately narrower than a new mutation-selection framework. Reversible origin-fixation weighting, weakest-link fitness, selection-versus-multiplicity effects, and representation-dependent evolutionary thresholds all have substantial prior art. Our result is instead the exact composition produced by one explicit finite representation: routing structure -> exact layer hierarchy -> one adjacent obstruction -> paired stationary occupancy thresholds. We also include representation and reversible mutation-measure counterexamples that state precisely where these thresholds cease to be identified.

We believe the manuscript fits *Theoretical Population Biology* because its main contribution is a mathematically explicit population-genetic result connecting genotype–phenotype representation to stationary population occupancy, while keeping the biological and modeling assumptions fully exposed.

All figures are generated reproducibly from the theorem implementation, and the complete computational audit is available in the accompanying repository. The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere.
