## Working title

**Exact occupancy thresholds in finite routing representations**

Alternatives:

- **Finite routing architecture sets exact thresholds for stationary gain occupancy**

Avoid titles built around “weakest-link epistasis”, “free fitness”, or generic “selection versus entropy”; those literatures predate this paper.

## Paper-level question

A finite routing architecture may have a unique maximally adapted genotype but many more genotypes at slightly lower gain. Two population-level questions are distinct:

1. how strong must selection be before the full-gain class becomes the **largest aggregate gain class**?
2. how strong must selection be before that class holds **at least half of stationary probability**?

The paper answers both questions for a declared finite branch-product routing representation, then shows exactly which conclusions disappear when the representation or neutral mutation measure is changed.

## One-sentence result

For

`X={0,...,q}^k`, `g(x)=min_i x_i`,

with symmetric per-gain stationary tilt `theta`, the full-gain class becomes aggregate-modal exactly at

`T_k=2^k-1`,

while its unique stationary-majority threshold `theta_1/2(q,k)` satisfies, for `q>=2`,

`T_k < theta_1/2(q,k) < 2T_k`.

For canonical routing `k=q+1` with Moran fitness step `a=2`, unique modality first occurs at `N=q+2` whereas stationary majority first occurs at `N=q+3` for every `q>=2`.

These are **aggregate gain-layer occupancy results**, not individual-genotype mode switches.

# Result hierarchy

The paper should contain four mathematical results and two scope controls. They are not six equal headline claims.

## Main Result 1 — exact finite routing-layer multiplicity

Let

`X_{q,k}={0,...,q}^k`,

and

`g(x)=min_i x_i`.

For gain layer `r=0,...,q`,

`D_r = (q-r+1)^k - (q-r)^k`.

Equivalently, if `s=q-r` is distance below full gain,

`A_s=(s+1)^k-s^k`.

The full-gain layer contains one genotype. The adjacent layer contains

`A_1=2^k-1`

genotypes.

### Role

This theorem turns the routing representation into an exact genotype multiplicity profile. The formula itself should not be advertised as a novel order-statistic identity; the contribution is its exact role inside the declared finite routing genotype-policy map.

## Main Result 2 — all lower layers reduce to the adjacent-layer obstruction

Define

`T_k=2^k-1`.

Then for every `s>=1`,

`A_s <= T_k^s`.

For `k>=2`, equality occurs only at `s=1`; for all `s>=2`,

`A_s < T_k^s`.

### Proof certificate

Map each `x in {0,...,s}^k` with `min_i x_i=0` to

`S_j={i:x_i<j}`, `j=1,...,s`.

The `S_j` are nonempty and nested. Removing the nesting constraint gives `(2^k-1)^s` arbitrary strings of nonempty subsets. At `s=1` there is no nesting restriction; for `k>=2,s>=2`, nonnested strings exist, giving strict inequality.

### Role

This is the combinatorial engine of the paper. It converts comparison against every lower gain layer into one adjacent-layer constant.

## Main Result 3 — exact aggregate-layer modality trichotomy

Under a symmetric stationary tilt `theta>0`, each genotype at gain `r` has weight proportional to `theta^r`, hence aggregate layer weight

`W_r=D_r theta^r`.

For `k>=2`:

1. `theta<T_k`: the full-gain layer is not aggregate-modal;
2. `theta=T_k`: exactly `{q-1,q}` tie for maximal aggregate stationary weight;
3. `theta>T_k`: the full-gain layer is uniquely aggregate-modal.

The proof follows from

`W_{q-s}/W_q = A_s/theta^s`.

### Role

This is the closed-form threshold result. It identifies when full gain becomes the largest aggregate gain class, but it does **not** identify stationary majority.

## Main Result 4 — exact stationary-majority boundary

The normalized full-gain stationary mass is

`P_full(theta)=1/(1+sum_{s=1}^q A_s theta^(-s))`.

Because this is strictly increasing in positive `theta`, there is a unique half-mass boundary `theta_1/2(q,k)` satisfying

`theta^q = sum_{s=1}^q A_s theta^(q-s)`.

For `q=1`,

`theta_1/2=T_k`.

For every `q>=2`,

`T_k < theta_1/2 < 2T_k`.

The strict lower inequality follows because at `theta=T_k` the adjacent layer alone ties the full layer and more distant layers contribute additional positive mass. The upper inequality follows from the global bound and the finite geometric sum

`sum_{s=1}^q (T_k/(2T_k))^s < 1`.

### Role

This removes the main ambiguity in interpreting aggregate modality. The paper now distinguishes the threshold for being the largest gain class from the stronger threshold for occupying at least half of stationarity.

# Population-size corollaries — prior-art bridge, not new machinery

For the declared Moran multiplicative fitness step `a>1`, standard origin-fixation theory gives

`theta=a^(N-1)`.

Therefore the mode and majority thresholds translate directly into population-size thresholds without changing the population-genetic theory.

For canonical routing

`k=q+1`

and `a=2`:

- first unique aggregate mode: `N=q+2`;
- first stationary majority: `N=q+3` for every `q>=2`;
- `q=1` is the boundary case where majority occurs already at `N=3=q+2`.

For `q=2,k=3`, the mode threshold is `theta=7`, while the majority threshold is the positive root of

`theta^2-7theta-19=0`,

namely `(7+5sqrt(5))/2`, between 9 and 10.

### Role

These corollaries turn architecture into a familiar finite-population scale while making clear that “largest class” and “majority occupancy” are distinct.

# Prior-art bridge — lemma, not contribution

The population-genetic machinery should be introduced briefly and explicitly as established prior art.

Under weak mutation and reversible neutral mutation measure `mu`, the selected stationary law has the standard form

`pi(x) proportional to mu(x) theta^g(x)`.

Under the symmetric neutral measure used for the main theorems, `mu` is constant on genotypes and layer mass is proportional to raw multiplicity times `theta^r`.

Do not present any of the following as new:

- Moran fixation probability;
- `rho(R)/rho(1/R)=R^(N-1)`;
- Sella–Hirsh / reversible stationary weighting;
- neutral-measure or sequence-entropy factors;
- equilibrium competition between selection and sequence multiplicity, including Riedel et al. (2015);
- minimum / weakest-link fitness maps;
- generic competition between state abundance and selection.

The closest contemporary comparison currently frozen in the novelty audit is Labourel, Bansept & McCandlish (2026), who analyze weakest-link `min` epistasis under mutation-selection-drift. The manuscript must distinguish their continuous component/order-statistic problem from the present finite routing-layer occupancy theorem.

# Scope Control 1 — representation dependence

Hold fixed:

- gain levels `{0,...,q}`;
- phenotype-level fitness `W(g)=a^g`;
- population-level tilt `theta`.

Compare:

### Branch-product representation

`X_prod={0,...,q}^{q+1}`, `g=min_i x_i`.

Mode threshold:

`theta_prod=2^(q+1)-1`.

### Compressed gain-chain representation

`X_chain={0,...,q}`, one genotype per gain.

Full gain is aggregate-modal for `theta>=1` and uniquely modal for `theta>1`.

For `q=2,theta=2`:

- compressed weights `(1,2,4)`, full mass `4/7`, full gain uniquely modal and a stationary majority;
- branch-product multiplicities `(19,7,1)`, weights `(19,14,4)`, full mass `4/37`, full gain nonmodal.

### Role

This prevents the paper from accidentally selling either threshold as a universal biological law. They are exact consequences of a declared representation.

# Scope Control 2 — mutation-bias / neutral-measure dependence

Even a fixed support graph does not identify stationary occupancy without the neutral mutation measure.

For reversible mutation measure `mu`,

`pi_x proportional to mu_x theta^g(x)`.

On a fixed gain path `0<->1<->...<->q`, any strictly positive target stationary distribution `p` can be realized by taking

`mu_r proportional to p_r theta^(-r)`

and constructing a reversible proposal kernel with that neutral measure.

### Role

This is a claim ceiling. It shows that raw multiplicity controls stationary occupancy only under the declared symmetric neutral measure. Do not promote generic mutation-bias nonidentifiability as a new theory of mutation bias.

# Main-text section sequence

## 1. Introduction

Motivate the gap among per-genotype ranking, aggregate gain-class modality, and stationary majority. State immediately that abundance/entropy effects, explicit multiplicity-selection equilibria, and weakest-link maps are established.

End with four questions:

1. How many genotypes occupy each routing gain layer?
2. Can all lower-layer comparisons be reduced to one obstruction?
3. At what exact tilt does full gain become the largest gain class?
4. When does full gain instead become a majority of stationary occupancy?

## 2. Model — finite routing genotype-policy map

Define `X_{q,k}`, `g=min`, symmetric stationary tilt, aggregate layer weights, full-layer mass, and the distinction among per-genotype mode, aggregate layer mode, and majority.

Keep Moran/Sella–Hirsh machinery to one compact prior-art subsection.

## 3. Exact layer multiplicity

Prove Main Result 1. Use `q=2,k=3` as the running example:

`D=(19,7,1)`.

## 4. A global combinatorial bound

Prove Main Result 2 with the nested-subset-chain certificate.

## 5. Two stationary occupancy transitions

First state the exact aggregate-mode trichotomy. Then derive the unique half-mass boundary and its factor-two bracket. Use the canonical `a=2` result `q+2` versus `q+3` as the biological/population-level interpretation.

## 6. The thresholds are representation-specific

Use the compressed chain counterexample.

## 7. The thresholds are mutation-measure-specific

State the reversible neutral-measure boundary and one fixed-support contradiction witness.

## 8. Discussion

Focus on five implications:

1. a unique optimum can be the most probable individual genotype before its phenotype class is the largest aggregate class;
2. the largest aggregate class can still fail to hold most stationary probability;
3. one adjacent-layer obstruction controls the complete lower-layer hierarchy;
4. representation and mutation measure are causal inputs, not nuisance implementation details;
5. biological application requires an empirically defensible genotype-policy map and mutation model.

# Biological claim ceiling

The paper may say:

> For a declared finite routing representation, genotype multiplicity creates exact architecture-dependent thresholds for becoming the largest gain class and for becoming a stationary majority.

It may not say:

> Natural regulatory, neural, or genetic routing systems generally have thresholds `2^k-1` or `theta_1/2(q,k)`.

Moving from the formal routing representation to a biological system requires independent evidence for the genotype-policy representation, local mutational moves, and neutral mutation measure.
