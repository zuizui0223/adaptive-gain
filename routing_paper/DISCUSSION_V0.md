# Discussion v0

The central result is a finite representation theorem. In the branch-product routing space `X={0,...,q}^k` with weakest-branch gain, the maximally adapted gain class contains a single genotype, whereas lower gain classes can contain many genotypes. That asymmetry creates an exact competition between selective tilt and representation-induced multiplicity. The competition is completely characterized by one architecture-dependent constant, `2^k-1`: below it the full-gain class is not the stationary aggregate mode, at equality it ties exactly with the adjacent class, and above it it becomes the unique aggregate mode.

This result is narrower than a general statement that genotype abundance can oppose selection. Such abundance–selection tradeoffs are already familiar from neutral-network, sequence-entropy, free-fitness, and survival-of-the-flattest perspectives. Likewise, a minimum across component performances is not new; limiting-factor models are longstanding, and recent weakest-link epistasis theory explicitly studies fitness determined by the least-fit phenotypic component. The contribution here is that one finite routing representation makes the multiplicity structure exactly countable and, more importantly, makes competition with **all** lower gain classes reducible to the multiplicity of the adjacent class.

That reduction is the main mathematical reason the threshold is sharp. The layer `s` steps below full gain contains `A_s=(s+1)^k-s^k` genotypes. Mapping those genotypes to nested sequences of nonempty branch subsets shows that `A_s` is bounded by `(2^k-1)^s`. The adjacent layer attains the bound, while every more distant layer is strictly below it for `k>=2`. Consequently the same quantity that determines whether the adjacent layer beats the optimum also controls every lower layer. The exact equality structure is informative: at the boundary there is no diffuse coexistence of many equally dominant gain classes; only the full and immediately adjacent classes tie.

The distinction between individual-genotype and aggregate-layer modality is essential for interpreting the result. Under the symmetric tilt, once `theta>1` the unique full-gain genotype already has greater stationary weight than any individual lower-gain genotype. The threshold `2^k-1` therefore does not mark a reversal in the rank of genotypes. It marks the point at which one highly weighted genotype overcomes the **combined** stationary mass of a more numerous gain class. This is why describing the result simply as an optimum becoming “the mode” would be misleading unless the object being aggregated is stated explicitly.

The Moran population-size corollary illustrates how the architecture result composes with established population genetics. Standard origin-fixation theory supplies the stationary tilt `theta=a^(N-1)` for the declared multiplicative fitness step. Substitution yields an exact condition on `N` without changing the underlying population-genetic theory. In the canonical construction `k=q+1` with `a=2`, the boundary `N=q+2` provides a compact example of how representational complexity can be translated into the amount of finite-population selection required for aggregate dominance. The interpretation should remain conditional: this is the population size implied by this genotype-policy representation and fitness-step model, not a universal effective-population-size law.

The representation contrast shows why that qualification is substantive rather than rhetorical. A compressed encoding with one genotype per gain level has the same gain values and can be assigned the same phenotype-level fitness schedule, yet its full-gain threshold is only `theta=1`. For `q=2` and `theta=2`, the compressed representation makes the full-gain class a stationary majority, whereas the branch-product representation makes it nonmodal. Nothing about the fitness ranking changed. The conclusion changed because the number and arrangement of genotypes realizing each gain changed. Thus a phenotype-level description is insufficient to recover the stationary result.

Mutation bias imposes a second and independent boundary. Even fixing the local mutation-support graph does not recover a unique stationary occupancy. Under reversible mutation, the neutral measure enters multiplicatively with the selective tilt, `pi(x) proportional to mu(x) theta^{g(x)}`. On a fixed path of gain levels, suitable reversible proposal weights can realize any strictly positive selected stationary distribution. The raw layer-count theorem therefore determines stationary occupancy only under the declared symmetric neutral measure. More generally, layer **neutral mass**, rather than genotype count alone, is the relevant multiplicity factor.

These two boundaries suggest a useful way to interpret finite genotype–phenotype models. Representation and mutation measure are not implementation details appended after the biological model has been specified. They are part of the causal specification that determines evolutionary accessibility and occupancy. A claim about a natural system therefore requires more than evidence that performance is weakest-link-like or state dependent. It requires a defensible mapping from heritable states to policy coordinates and a defensible model of which local changes occur with what neutral weights.

The present theory deliberately stops before making that biological identification. The branch-product routing genotype is a declared formal representation. It may provide a useful abstraction for systems in which multiple heritable modules must all reach adequate state-dependent performance, but structural analogy is not enough to establish that a real neural, regulatory, or developmental system has genotype space `{0,...,q}^k`, local coordinate mutations, or a symmetric neutral measure. The ongoing biological qualification work should therefore be viewed as an independent test of whether any empirical system licenses this representation, not as evidence silently assumed by the theorem.

Several extensions are mathematically possible but are not necessary for the present paper. One could analyze acquisition times on neutral plateaus, mesoscopic approach to the full target, absolute mutation-rate scaling, or downstream population processes. Those questions concern accessibility and time rather than the stationary layer transition proved here. Including them would weaken the paper’s central distinction between what is exactly determined by finite routing multiplicity and what requires additional representation or process assumptions.

The broader implication is consequently modest but precise. Finite architecture can convert a simple monotone fitness schedule into a nontrivial stationary phenotype distribution because representation determines how much genotype mass sits behind each phenotype. In the branch-product routing model, that effect is unusually tractable: one can count every layer exactly, prove a global multiplicity bound, and identify the unique sharp boundary at which the fully adapted gain class overtakes its combinatorial competitors. The value of the result lies less in asserting a universal law than in showing how explicit representation turns a vague selection-versus-multiplicity intuition into an exact and falsifiable conditional statement.

## Discussion claims that must survive revision

1. The contribution is finite routing combinatorics plus its exact aggregate-layer consequence, not generic weakest-link epistasis or mutation-selection balance.
2. `2^k-1` is an aggregate-layer threshold, never an individual-genotype threshold.
3. Representation dependence is a main scope control because it demonstrates that the threshold is not identified by phenotype fitness alone.
4. Mutation-measure dependence is a separate scope control because fixed support still does not identify occupancy.
5. Biological application requires independent genotype-policy and mutation qualification.
6. Waiting-time and downstream-process results remain outside the main paper.
