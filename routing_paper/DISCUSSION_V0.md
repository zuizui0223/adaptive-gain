# Discussion v0

The central result is a finite representation theorem about **stationary occupancy at two distinct aggregation levels**. In the branch-product routing space `X={0,...,q}^k` with weakest-branch gain, the maximally adapted gain class contains a single genotype, whereas lower gain classes can contain many genotypes. That asymmetry creates an exact competition between selective tilt and representation-induced multiplicity. The first transition is closed form: at `T_k=2^k-1` the full-gain class first becomes an aggregate mode. The second is stronger: the full-gain class becomes a stationary majority only at the unique half-mass threshold `theta_1/2(q,k)`, which for `q>=2` lies strictly between `T_k` and `2T_k`.

This two-threshold structure matters because “largest class” and “most of the stationary probability” are not the same evolutionary statement. At the modality threshold, the full and adjacent layers tie while more distant layers still carry positive mass. In the canonical `q=2,k=3` example, `theta=7` gives weights `(19,49,49)`, so full gain is modal but has stationary mass only `49/117`. Its majority boundary instead solves `theta^2-7theta-19=0`, giving `(7+5sqrt(5))/2`, between 9 and 10. The exact mode theorem is therefore not being used as a proxy for majority; the paper treats the two estimands separately.

The broader abundance–selection principle is not new. Neutral-network, sequence-entropy, free-fitness, survival-of-the-flattest, and explicit multiplicity-selection equilibrium models already show that the number of sequence states representing a phenotype can alter stationary outcomes. Riedel et al. (2015), for example, include sequence multiplicity directly alongside selection in low-mutation equilibrium statistics. Likewise, minimum or weakest-link phenotype maps are longstanding, and recent weakest-link epistasis work explicitly studies mutation–selection–drift when organismal fitness is set by the least-fit component. The contribution here is narrower: one finite routing representation makes the multiplicity hierarchy exactly countable and makes comparison with **all** lower gain classes reducible to one adjacent-layer obstruction.

That reduction is the main mathematical reason the mode threshold is sharp. The layer `s` steps below full gain contains `A_s=(s+1)^k-s^k` genotypes. Mapping those genotypes to nested sequences of nonempty branch subsets shows that `A_s` is bounded by `(2^k-1)^s`. The adjacent layer attains the bound, while every more distant layer is strictly below it for `k>=2`. Consequently the same quantity that determines whether the adjacent layer beats the optimum also controls every lower layer. At equality there is no diffuse coexistence of many equally maximal gain classes: only the full and immediately adjacent classes tie.

The same global bound also explains why stationary majority is nearby but not identical. Full-gain mass is

`P_full(theta)=1/(1+sum_{s=1}^q A_s theta^(-s))`.

At `theta=T_k` the adjacent term alone equals the full-layer contribution, so any additional lower layer makes majority impossible when `q>=2`. At `theta=2T_k`, the lower/full ratio is bounded by the finite geometric sum `sum_{s=1}^q 2^{-s}<1`, so majority has already been reached. Thus one combinatorial certificate produces both an exact modal threshold and a factor-two bracket for the unique majority threshold.

The distinction among per-genotype mode, aggregate-layer mode, and stationary majority is essential. Under the symmetric tilt, once `theta>1` the unique full-gain genotype already has greater stationary weight than any individual lower-gain genotype. The factor `2^k-1` appears only after summing over all genotypes in a gain layer. A second increase in tilt is then required, for `q>=2`, before the full-gain layer contains at least half of the stationary probability. These three objects should not be collapsed into the word “dominance.”

The Moran population-size corollaries illustrate how the architecture result composes with established population genetics. Standard origin-fixation theory supplies `theta=a^(N-1)` for the declared multiplicative fitness step. In canonical routing `k=q+1` with `a=2`, the full-gain class first becomes the unique aggregate mode at `N=q+2`, but for every `q>=2` it first becomes a stationary majority at `N=q+3`. This one-step separation is exact. It converts a representational distinction into an immediately interpretable finite-population contrast without making the Moran machinery itself a contribution.

The representation contrast shows why these thresholds are conditional rather than universal. A compressed encoding with one genotype per gain level has the same gain values and can be assigned the same phenotype-level fitness schedule, yet its full-gain aggregate-mode threshold is only `theta=1`. For `q=2` and `theta=2`, the compressed representation makes the full-gain class both uniquely modal and a stationary majority, whereas the branch-product representation gives full mass `4/37` and makes full gain nonmodal. Nothing about the phenotype fitness ranking changed. The conclusion changed because the number and arrangement of genotypes realizing each gain changed.

Mutation bias imposes a second and independent boundary. Even fixing the local mutation-support graph does not recover a unique stationary occupancy. Under reversible mutation, the neutral measure enters multiplicatively with the selective tilt, `pi(x) proportional to mu(x) theta^{g(x)}`. On a fixed path of gain levels, suitable reversible proposal weights can realize any strictly positive selected stationary distribution. Raw layer counts therefore control occupancy only under the declared symmetric neutral measure. More generally, layer **neutral mass**, rather than genotype count alone, is the relevant abundance factor.

These two boundaries suggest a useful interpretation of finite genotype–phenotype models. Representation and mutation measure are not implementation details appended after the biological model has been specified. They are part of the causal specification that determines evolutionary occupancy. A claim about a natural system therefore requires more than evidence that performance is weakest-link-like or state dependent. It requires a defensible mapping from heritable states to policy coordinates and a defensible model of which local changes occur with what neutral weights.

The present theory deliberately stops before making that biological identification. The branch-product routing genotype is a declared formal representation. It may provide a useful abstraction for systems in which multiple heritable modules must all reach adequate state-dependent performance, but structural analogy is not enough to establish that a real neural, regulatory, or developmental system has genotype space `{0,...,q}^k`, local coordinate mutations, or a symmetric neutral measure. Biological qualification must therefore remain independent of the theorem.

Several extensions are mathematically possible but are not necessary for the present paper. Neutral-plateau acquisition times, mesoscopic approach to the full target, absolute mutation-rate scaling, and downstream population processes concern accessibility and time rather than the two stationary occupancy transitions proved here. Including them would turn a compact representation theorem into a history of the repository's side branches.

The broader implication is modest but precise. Finite architecture can convert a simple monotone fitness schedule into a nontrivial stationary phenotype distribution because representation determines how much genotype mass sits behind each phenotype. In the branch-product routing model, this effect is unusually tractable: every gain layer can be counted exactly, one adjacent-layer constant controls the complete lower-layer hierarchy, and the theory separates the point at which the optimum becomes the largest gain class from the stronger point at which it becomes most of stationary occupancy. The value of the result lies less in asserting a universal law than in showing how explicit representation turns a vague selection-versus-multiplicity intuition into exact, assumption-explicit thresholds.

## Discussion claims that must survive revision

1. The contribution is finite routing combinatorics plus its exact occupancy consequences, not generic weakest-link epistasis or mutation-selection balance.
2. `2^k-1` is an aggregate-layer modality threshold, never an individual-genotype or majority threshold.
3. For `q>=2`, the stationary-majority threshold is strictly larger and strictly less than twice the modality threshold.
4. Canonical `a=2` routing separates unique mode (`N=q+2`) from majority (`N=q+3`) for `q>=2`.
5. Representation dependence is a main scope control because phenotype fitness alone does not identify either stationary conclusion.
6. Mutation-measure dependence is a separate scope control because fixed support still does not identify occupancy.
7. Biological application requires independent genotype-policy and mutation qualification.
8. Waiting-time and downstream-process results remain outside the main paper.
