## Figure 1. Finite decision geometry exposes ecological selection windows and dynamical no-go regions

**A**, for the same deterministic target-resolution problem, the optimal adaptive worst-path acquisition cost cannot exceed the optimal fixed resolving-bundle cost, `C_A<=C_F`. A hard ecological budget `B` therefore has three exact regions: below `C_A` neither architecture guarantees resolution; for `C_A<=B<C_F` only contingent sensing guarantees resolution; and at or above `C_F` both do. **B**, under the declared threshold fitness model, contingent architecture receives the resolution benefit only in the adaptive-only interval. Its selection coefficient there is `s_B=log[(w_0+v)/w_0]-kappa`, while outside the interval the declared resolution-only difference is `-kappa`. This interpretation requires a biologically justified hard time, energy, exposure, handling, developmental or opportunity budget. **C**, ecological states also differ in their structural gap `g_i=C_F(i)-C_A(i)`. For ordered contrast `Delta g=g_2-g_1` and any nondecreasing sensing-to-selection lift with marginal effect bounded by `L`, local feedback is bounded by `G<=B_f L Delta g`, where `B_f` is feedback per unit selection. If `B_f L Delta g_max<=G_osc`, the requested oscillatory regime is unreachable for every lift in the declared class. Crossing this bound means only that oscillation is not ruled out; it does not guarantee oscillation.

**Alt text:** Three-panel schematic linking exact finite decision costs to ecological selection and feedback. Panel A shows an ecological budget axis partitioned at adaptive cost C_A and larger or equal fixed cost C_F: both strategies fail below C_A, only adaptive sensing succeeds between C_A and C_F, and both succeed above C_F. Panel B shows that a threshold fitness benefit of resolving the target is gained only in the adaptive-only interval, conditional on a hard ecological budget. Panel C compares state-specific structural gaps across two ecological states and shows the nonlinear upper bound G less than or equal to B_f times L times Delta g; architectures whose maximum allowed feedback remains below the oscillation threshold are labeled unreachable, whereas crossing the threshold is labeled not ruled out rather than guaranteed.

## Figure 2. Sharp finite requirements and balanced cues reveal the geometry controlling adaptive value

**A**, a required integer structural gap `q` forces at least one ecological state to contain a gap-capable finite task. For binary unit-cost sensing, with `h_2^*(q)=min{h>=1:2^h-1-h>=q}`, the exact first componentwise corner is `(n^*,m^*,E^*)=(h_2^*+q+1,h_2^*+q,h_2^*+q)`. The canonical `q=2` example gives `(6,5,5)`. **B**, for bounded cue arity greater than two, joint requirements are generally Pareto-valued rather than one scalar minimum. At `q=3,b=4`, `(7,6,6)` and `(8,5,5)` are both nondominated, exposing a tradeoff between represented alternatives and cue/frontier burden. **C**, marginal cue balance does not bound adaptive advantage. There exists a deterministic binary unit-cost family in which every query is exactly 50/50 balanced over represented worlds, yet branch-specific terminal resources force `C_F>=2^d` while an adaptive policy uses at most `d+1` queries, so `C_F/C_A>=2^d/(d+1)` diverges. This is an existence result, not a sharp maximum for fixed `(n,m)`.

**Alt text:** Three-panel schematic of finite structural constraints. Panel A maps a required gap q to the exact binary minimum represented-world, query and productive-obligation counts, highlighting q equals two and the corner six, five, five. Panel B shows two nondominated bounded-arity solutions, seven-six-six and eight-five-five, to emphasize that finite sensing complexity is Pareto-valued. Panel C depicts a routing tree whose binary queries are globally balanced fifty-fifty but lead to branch-specific terminal cues. The fixed architecture must carry exponentially many terminal resources while the adaptive architecture follows routing queries then one terminal cue, producing an unbounded fixed-to-adaptive cost ratio. The panel is labeled as a family-level existence result rather than a fixed-size sharp maximum.

## Figure 3. Ecology filters structurally generated selection through time

**A**, each recurrent ecological state carries a target-relevant finite sensing problem and therefore a state-specific structural reward or selection contribution. The raw number of ecological distinctions need not equal the decision-relevant state count because same-target worlds or dominated cues can sometimes be quotient-reduced without changing the exact adaptive optimum. **B**, two ecological processes can have equally persistent slow modes yet different evolutionary consequences when the structurally generated reward vector aligns differently with those modes. **C**, for a finite ergodic reversible ecological transition process, `sigma_eff^2=sum_r w_r(1+r_r)/(1-r_r)`, separating temporal amplification by ecological persistence from structural reward-mode alignment `w_r`. Ecological persistence alone does not determine long-run evolutionary fluctuation.

**Alt text:** Three-panel schematic of temporal filtering. Panel A shows recurrent ecological states carrying finite sensing problems and state-specific selection rewards, with a note that exact target-relevant quotienting can remove decision-irrelevant raw distinctions. Panel B compares two systems with the same slow ecological persistence but different alignment between the structural reward vector and the slow mode, producing different evolutionary consequences. Panel C shows the reversible-chain spectral expression for long-run variance as a sum of reward weights times persistence amplification. The figure emphasizes that persistence alone is insufficient without reward-mode alignment.

## Figure-level claim firewall

These legends are intentionally asymmetric in strength. Figure 1 contains the paper's selection-window and no-go conclusions. Figure 2 supplies sharp finite geometry and a structural counterexample to marginal-balance intuition. Figure 3 supplies a temporal filter, not a second reachability theorem. No figure claims empirical values of `B`, `B_f`, `L`, `alpha`, `phi`, `w_0`, `v` or `kappa` for a natural population.
