## Figure 1. Ecological deadlines expose the value of contingent sensing

**A**, a natural-history decision can contain conditionally relevant information. An organism encounters a resource, host, predator or other focal situation, samples an early cue, and either acts immediately or continues to a branch-specific cue that is useful only after a particular early outcome. The ecological value of the early cue can therefore be routing rather than direct determination of the final action. **B**, for the same declared decision target, the minimum cost of a contingent cue sequence, `C_A`, cannot exceed the minimum cost of a precommitted resolving cue set, `C_F`. A hard or effectively hard ecological budget `B` therefore defines three regions: neither architecture guarantees resolution when `B<C_A`; only contingent sensing does so when `C_A<=B<C_F`; and both do so when `B>=C_F`. The intermediate region is a unique performance opportunity for contingent sensing and produces positive selection under the declared threshold model only when the benefit of timely resolution exceeds the maintenance cost of contingent control. **C**, the resulting ecological prediction is non-monotonic: contingent sensing has no unique performance advantage when constraint is either too severe or sufficiently weak, and is exposed only at intermediate constraint.

**Alt text:** Three-panel ecological schematic. Panel A shows an encounter followed by an early cue that either ends the decision or routes the organism to a branch-specific later cue, emphasizing conditional cue relevance. Panel B shows a budget axis divided at the contingent cost C_A and the larger or equal fixed benchmark C_F: neither strategy succeeds below C_A, only contingent sensing succeeds between C_A and C_F, and both succeed above C_F. Panel C shows a conceptual zero–positive–zero pattern in the unique advantage of contingent sensing across increasing available budget. The figure states that the middle region creates a performance opportunity and yields positive selection only when resolution benefit exceeds maintenance cost.

## Figure 2. Conditional cue dependence—not cue prevalence—creates contingent value

**A**, two decision systems can have similar one-cue statistics yet different conditional structure. In the shared system, later cues remain useful across branches. In the routed system, an early cue directs the organism toward one branch-specific terminal cue. **B**, a non-contingent fixed benchmark must provision every potentially relevant terminal cue before branch identity is known, whereas a contingent strategy pays for routing and then only the terminal cue needed on the realized branch. The repository contains an exactly balanced binary family in which this branch-exclusive structure produces an unbounded fixed-to-contingent cost ratio as problem size grows; the figure uses only the ecological mechanism, not the full extremal construction. **C**, raw cue count and marginal cue balance are therefore weak or insufficient proxies for contingent value. The mechanistic predictor within the declared finite model is conditional cue dependence together with acquisition cost: which cue becomes useful after which earlier observation.

**Alt text:** Three-panel schematic comparing cue structures. Panel A contrasts a system in which the same later cues are useful in both branches with a routed system in which different terminal cues are needed after different early outcomes, despite similar marginal cue statistics. Panel B contrasts fixed provisioning of all potentially relevant terminal cues with contingent routing followed by only one branch-specific terminal cue. Panel C ranks raw cue count and marginal cue balance as poor proxies and highlights conditional cue dependence and acquisition cost as the mechanistic predictors. The figure does not claim a universal scalar measure of ecological complexity.

## Figure 3. Environmental persistence matters only when sensing selection aligns with it

**A**, recurrent ecological states such as seasons, habitats or community contexts can carry different state-specific payoffs to contingent sensing because they change the focal decision problem or its budget. **B**, two ecological systems can share the same slow environmental mode but differ in evolutionary consequence. When state-specific sensing selection aligns with that slow mode, long-run amplification is strong; when the same selection contrast is nearly orthogonal to the slow mode, persistence alone contributes little. **C**, for the declared finite ergodic reversible recurrence model, (sigma_{m eff}^2=sum_r w_r(1+r_r)/(1-r_r)), where `r_r` is ecological persistence and `w_r` is the loading of sensing-generated selection on that ecological mode. The prediction is therefore an interaction between ecological persistence and payoff-mode alignment, not a main effect of environmental autocorrelation alone.

**Alt text:** Three-panel schematic of temporal ecology. Panel A shows recurrent season, habitat and community states with different contingent-sensing payoffs. Panel B compares two systems with the same slow ecological mode but different alignment between that mode and the state-specific selection contrast, producing strong versus weak long-run amplification. Panel C summarizes the long-run effect as the joint action of ecological persistence and payoff-mode alignment, with the formal spectral expression left to the Supplement. The figure emphasizes that environmental autocorrelation alone is insufficient.

## Figure-level claim firewall

- Natural-history systems in the figures are generic illustrations rather than fitted empirical examples.
- `C_F` is a counterfactual non-contingent benchmark and does not imply simultaneous sampling by real organisms.
- The middle budget interval is a unique performance opportunity; positive selection additionally requires the declared benefit–maintenance condition.
- The balanced-family result supports the insufficiency of marginal cue balance; it is not presented as a universal fixed-size optimum.
- Conditional cue dependence is a mechanistic predictor within the finite deterministic model, not a universal scalar measure of ecological complexity.
- Figure 3 is a temporal filter, not an independent causal proof that sensing generates observed ecological cycles.
