# Evolution Letters figure legends v1

## Figure 1. Finite sensing architecture creates a dynamical no-go region

**A**, Outcome-contingent cue acquisition can reduce guaranteed cue burden relative to a fixed resolving bundle, producing structural gap `Delta g = C_F - C_A`. **B**, the higher-tier theorem does not require exact linearity between structural gap and selection. For any nondecreasing sensing-to-selection lift with marginal effect bounded by `L`, the generated selection contrast satisfies `Delta s <= L Delta g`, and feedback is bounded by `G <= B L Delta g` for positive feedback-per-selection scale `B`. **C**, if even the maximum allowed feedback at an architecture's gap ceiling fails to exceed `G_osc`, the oscillatory regime is unreachable for every lift in the declared monotone-Lipschitz class. Crossing the bound means only that the regime is not ruled out; it does not guarantee oscillation.

## Figure 2. Required dynamics imply finite natural-history architecture

A required local dynamical regime first imposes a required structural gap and then a finite sensing-architecture requirement. **A**, under binary unit-cost sensing, required gap `q=2` has exact first componentwise corner `(n,m,E)=(6,5,5)`, where `n` is represented alternatives, `m` cue resources and `E` irreducible fixed-side obligations. **B**, in the canonical nonlinear no-go example, all binary tasks with `n<=5`, `m<=4`, or `E<=4` have `Delta g<=1` and therefore cannot reach a regime requiring gap at least two. **C**, with bounded arity greater than two the exact requirement is generally Pareto-valued rather than one scalar minimum; for `q=3, b=4`, `(7,6,6)` and `(8,5,5)` are both nondominated.

## Figure 3. Ecology filters structurally generated selection through time

**A**, each recurrent ecological state carries both a finite sensing architecture, which generates state-indexed structural reward/selection, and a transition process that determines when the same reward recurs. **B**, slow ecological modes have large long-run evolutionary consequences only when the structurally generated reward vector aligns with those modes. **C**, for a reversible ecological transition process, `sigma_eff^2 = sum_r w_r (1+r_r)/(1-r_r)`, separating temporal amplification by ecological persistence from reward-mode alignment `w_r`. Thus ecological persistence alone does not determine evolutionary timescale or cumulative effect.
