# Figure legends — Evolution Letters V6 fitness frontier

## Figure 1. Natural history re-ranks an exact finite information frontier

**A**, finite world, query and query-arity constraints generate an exact
attainable frontier of adaptive worst-path cost (C_A) and fixed resolving
cost (C_F). For the binary (n=10,m=9) example, highlighted frontier points
include ((2,3)), ((3,7)) and ((4,9)). **B**, the structural
fixed-to-contingent ratio is largest at adaptive depth three. **C**, when
completion value decays exponentially through time, the biologically optimal
frontier point changes with ecological urgency: the optimum moves from depth
four to depth three to depth two. The structural frontier is unchanged;
natural history changes its ranking. Structural ratios are not interpreted as
fitness.

**Alt text:** Three panels show an exact frontier of contingent and fixed
information costs, the structural ratio ranking of three frontier points, and
three ecological-value curves crossing as urgency changes. The panel
emphasizes that the structural ratio optimum differs from the biological-value
optimum.

## Figure 2. Information branching imposes exact robust evolutionary limits

**A**, architecture cost and the biological value of completion determine the
fixed information burden required for contingent sensing to have positive
statewise value at each adaptive depth. **B**, with exponential completion
value (U(c)=e^{-0.3c}), the maximum robustly payable control cost rises with
allowed cue arity. A cost of (K=0.30) lies above the global binary ceiling but
below the ternary ceiling, giving a minimum robust cue arity of three in the
declared model. **C**, no-go constraints can arise from finite world/query
budgets, from bounded query arity even when task size is unlimited, or from the
natural-history value remaining after the minimum strict adaptive decision
depth. Cue arity denotes the number of distinguishable outcomes of one query,
not biological receptor count.

**Alt text:** Three panels move from a depth-specific required information
burden to a curve of robust control-cost ceiling against query arity and then to
a hierarchy of finite-resource, bounded-arity and unrestricted-information
limits.

## Figure 3. Encounter frequencies create an expected-value regime beyond the robust frontier

**A**, the absolute robust statewise value ceiling is
(U(2)-U_infty), whereas the expected-value ceiling is
(U(1)-U_infty). The interval between them is a frequency-assisted
evolvability band. **B**, at fixed finite (n=10,m=9), a binary tree can place
a common state in a one-query branch while rare states carry the fixed
information burden. Expected value rises with the encounter mass on the
one-query branch and crosses the declared architecture cost at a finite
threshold. **C**, public Aedes aegypti source data provide a temporal
process-shape anchor: after transient CO2, half of the aggregate infrared
advantage accumulates only after approximately 46 s in each post-pulse period.
This empirical panel demonstrates nonuniform timing of a cue effect; it is not
an estimate of fitness, natural opportunity survival or adaptive-versus-fixed
sensing.

**Alt text:** Panel A compares robust and expected evolutionary-value ceilings.
Panel B shows a binary decision tree with a common shallow branch and rare deep
branches plus a threshold curve against common-state encounter frequency.
Panel C shows delayed accumulation of the public Aedes infrared behavioral
effect through post-pulse time.
