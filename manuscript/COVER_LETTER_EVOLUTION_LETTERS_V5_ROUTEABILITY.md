Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Environmental routeability determines the ecological cost of heterogeneity,”** for publication as a Letter in *Evolution Letters*.

Environmental heterogeneity is central to ecological and evolutionary theory because it is expected to shape niche breadth, adaptive behavior, interaction structure and the maintenance of diversity. Yet heterogeneity is usually described by how many ecological alternatives exist or how strongly environments vary. Our manuscript asks a different question: when does that diversity actually become complex for an ecological interaction?

We introduce **environmental routeability**, the task-specific extent to which early cues partition ecological alternatives so that later information is needed only within the realized branch. The distinction is environmental rather than merely behavioral. Two communities can contain the same number of resources and identical marginal cue frequencies yet impose radically different information costs because their alternatives are organized differently.

The theory yields three broad ecological conclusions. First, raw richness and effective ecological complexity can be decoupled: adding species or environmental states need not increase decision burden when those additions remain within the same decision-equivalence class. Second, routeable heterogeneity can lower the information cost of broad niche use, refining the common expectation that more heterogeneous environments favor generalism or flexibility. Third, routeability can constrain the difference between a community’s potential interaction network and the subset of interactions that is behaviorally accessible within finite ecological budgets. Temporal heterogeneity is similarly filtered: persistence matters when recurrent states differ in interaction-relevant structure, not through autocorrelation alone.

These conclusions build directly on established work on heterogeneous environments, decision ecology, sequential host cues and adaptive foraging, but identify a structural property that those frameworks do not isolate. The manuscript therefore shifts the emphasis from “how much heterogeneity is present?” to “how is heterogeneity organized for ecological interaction?”

The exact finite mathematics, proofs and claim boundaries are retained in the Supplement. The main Letter is intentionally ecological: it centers on biodiversity versus effective complexity, niche breadth, potential versus accessible interaction networks, and the temporal structure of environmental heterogeneity. No empirical system is presented as a fitted demonstration; natural-history examples are used only to interpret the theory.

All supporting source code and mathematical validation are publicly available in the `zuizui0223/adaptive-gain` repository. A permanent archival identifier will be supplied at submission if available.
