# Finite information structure constrains evolutionary timescales and feedback phases

## Status

Draft manuscript scaffold for the eco-evolutionary synthesis on `theory/general-response-identifiability`.

This file is deliberately narrower than the full repository. The paper-level claim is not that rapid short-term evolution can coexist with long-term stasis. That is prior art. It is also not that animals use information adaptively, that adaptive decision trees can outperform fixed test sets, or that eco-evolutionary feedback can generate restoration or oscillation. Those are established ideas in evolutionary ecology, decision-tree/test-cover theory, and dynamical systems. The paper asks instead when finite individual information structure constrains the amplitude, persistence, and dynamical phase of evolutionary change.

## Abstract — working draft

Rapid evolutionary change over short intervals can coexist with little net change over long intervals, but fluctuating-selection and quantitative-genetic theory already explain why temporal cancellation can produce this pattern. Information acquisition is likewise an established adaptive-decision problem in evolutionary ecology, while fixed separating test sets and adaptive decision trees are classical objects in combinatorial optimization and test theory. Here we ask a different question: when selection is generated by finite information use, which evolutionary amplitudes and temporal regimes are structurally reachable in the first place? We represent each recurrent community state by a finite sensing task and distinguish the optimal adaptive cost from the corresponding fixed information cost. Their structural gap generates state-dependent selection, while transitions among the same community states generate temporal recurrence. This common state space couples selection amplitude to temporal filtering rather than treating them as independent phenomenological inputs. We derive four results. First, finite structural range and community persistence jointly impose a sharp ceiling on long-run evolutionary fluctuation. Second, a required dynamical gain implies a minimum, or under bounded query arity a Pareto-minimal, finite information structure; productive-frontier obligations therefore survive as a downstream ecological coordinate rather than an internal combinatorial artifact. Third, long-term stasis has two dynamically distinct origins: neutral cancellation under temporally opposing selection and attractive restoration under endogenous eco-evolutionary feedback. Fourth, within the generalized local model, monotone return may admit a zero-feedback decomposition whereas a complex local eigenpair forces positive feedback existence even though its magnitude remains unidentified. The resulting synthesis links individual information use, community recurrence, and evolutionary time by showing that finite information structure restricts which evolutionary timescales and feedback phases are reachable.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Known background that must not be claimed as novelty

Strong selection and rapid phenotypic evolution can occur over ecological timescales, and ecological and evolutionary dynamics can therefore overlap in time. Temporal changes in the direction of selection can also reconcile rapid short-term evolutionary activity with much weaker long-term net divergence. These points are established by prior work on rapid evolution, fluctuating selection, and the paradox of stasis.

Information acquisition and use are also established topics in evolutionary ecology. Animals can reduce ecological uncertainty through information gathering, and statistical decision theory has already been proposed as a quantitative framework for analysing the utility of information. Subsequent ecology-of-information work explicitly connects acquisition, decision making, and ecological consequences. Repeated sampling, state-dependent cue use, and feedback between behaviour and subsequent information acquisition therefore belong to the background rather than the novelty claim.

Likewise, the mathematical primitives on the fixed and adaptive sides are not new. Minimum test set/test cover asks for a smallest fixed collection of tests that distinguishes all items, while optimal decision-tree theory asks how tests should be chosen adaptively to identify an unknown item. Generic adaptive advantage, adaptivity gaps, decision-tree complexity, and test-cover complexity are established computer-science/test-theory topics. We use this machinery as an upstream structural coordinate rather than claiming it as a new algorithmic problem.

### 1.2 Gap addressed here

The novelty candidate is not `adaptive is better than fixed`. Nor is it `information matters`, `autocorrelation matters`, or `feedback can oscillate`. The paper composes these previously separate levels into a reachability problem.

Standard fluctuating-selection formulations can specify the amplitude of selection and its temporal autocorrelation as separate inputs. The present framework instead generates both on the same recurrent community-state space:

- a finite sensing task at state `i` generates the structural reward `g_i = C_F(i)-C_A(i)`;
- the community transition operator `P` determines how those same rewards recur through time.

The paper therefore asks:

> **When individual information use generates state-dependent selection, how does finite information structure constrain both the amplitude and the temporal fate of evolutionary change?**

The strongest defensible contribution statement at this stage is:

> Evolutionary ecology already treats information acquisition as an adaptive decision problem, while computer science and test theory separately characterize the costs of fixed separating test sets and adaptive decision trees. Fluctuating selection and eco-evolutionary feedback likewise already explain how rapid short-term change can fail to accumulate or can be dynamically restored. Our contribution lies in composing these previously separate levels. We show that finite information structure places exact ceilings on structurally generated selection, that those ceilings combine with community persistence to bound long-run evolutionary fluctuation, and that a required feedback regime implies a minimum or Pareto-minimal finite information structure. Thus the combinatorial structure of individual information use restricts which evolutionary timescales and local feedback phases are reachable.

Do not strengthen this to a `first-ever` claim until the remaining exact prior-art audit is complete.

## 2. Model

### 2.1 Finite sensing structure within a community state

For recurrent community state `i`, define a finite deterministic sensing task with adaptive cost `C_A(i)` and fixed cost `C_F(i)`. The structural gap is

\[
g_i=C_F(i)-C_A(i).
\]

The terms `fixed test set` and `adaptive decision tree` intentionally overlap established test-cover and decision-tree theory. Their use here is ecological: the gap is lifted into a state-dependent selection or feedback coordinate and then constrained by the finite sensing extremal theory.

A declared ecological lift maps structure to selection,

\[
s_i=\lambda g_i-\kappa,
\]

or, for local endogenous feedback,

\[
G=a\Delta g.
\]

The lift is an explicit modelling assumption; no claim is made that the combinatorial gap is itself fitness.

### 2.2 Community recurrence

Let `P` be a finite ergodic community transition matrix with stationary distribution `pi`. The reward vector `s` and transition operator `P` live on the same state space. For reversible `P`, the long-run fluctuation strength is expressed through the projections of the structurally generated reward onto community relaxation modes.

### 2.3 Endogenous local response

Use the generalized two-coordinate local response with evolutionary persistence `alpha`, community persistence `phi`, and feedback gain `G`. Its local invariants are

\[
T=\alpha+\phi,
\qquad
D=\alpha\phi+(1-\phi)G.
\]

Keep inverse nonidentifiability only as a time-interpretation limit. No observation-design program belongs in this paper.

## 3. Results

### Result 1. Structural-temporal joint ceiling

If recurrent structural gaps satisfy

\[
0\le g_i\le g_{\max},
\]

and the community chain is finite, ergodic, and reversible with largest nontrivial eigenvalue `r_max<1`, then

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{\rm eff}^2
\le
\frac{(\lambda g_{\max})^2}{4}
\frac{1+r_{\max}}{1-r_{\max}}.
}
\]

A symmetric two-state chain with endpoint rewards attains equality.

Interpretation: structural amplitude and temporal persistence jointly bound long-run evolutionary fluctuation. Slow community modes matter only when the structurally generated reward projects onto them.

Corollary for nonzero stationary mean-selection magnitude `|mu|`:

\[
H_{\times}^{\rm asy}
\le
\frac{(\lambda g_{\max})^2}{4\mu^2}
\frac{1+r_{\max}}{1-r_{\max}}.
\]

This is an asymptotic crossover proxy, not a finite-time hitting-time theorem.

### Result 2. Required dynamics imply minimum information complexity

Suppose a dynamical regime requires integer structural gap `q>=1`.

For binary unit-cost sensing,

\[
h_2^*(q)=\min\{h\ge1:2^h-1-h\ge q\},
\]

and the exact first corner is

\[
\boxed{
(n^*,m^*,E^*)
=
(h_2^*+q+1,\ h_2^*+q,\ h_2^*+q),
}
\]

where `E=|H_min|` is the minimal productive-frontier obligation count.

For bounded arity `b>=2`,

\[
h_b^*(q)=
\min\left\{h\ge1:\frac{b^h-1}{b-1}-h\ge q\right\},
\]

with

\[
m_{\min}=E_{\min}=q+h_b^*(q),
\]

while

\[
n_{\min}=q+h_2^*(q)+1.
\]

The exact joint requirement is therefore generally a Pareto frontier `P_b(q)`, not a single componentwise minimum. The dynamic composition is

\[
\boxed{
(\alpha,\phi,a,b)
\longrightarrow q_{\rm osc}
\longrightarrow \mathcal P_b(q_{\rm osc}).
}
\]

Canonical binary corollary: under `alpha=1`, `phi=1/2`, `a=1/8`, the first stable oscillatory gap is `q_osc=2`, giving `(n,m,E)=(6,5,5)`.

### Result 3. Two origins of stasis

For additive periodic weak selection

\[
z_{t+1}=z_t+E\beta_t,
\]

if one period sums to zero, then

\[
F_P(z)=z
\]

with multiplier one. This is neutral cancellation stasis: evolutionary activity can be arbitrarily large within the period, but perturbations are not restored.

By contrast, an endogenous equilibrium with

\[
\rho(J)<1
\]

is locally attractive. This is restoring stasis: perturbations decay.

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{cancellation stasis} &= \text{zero period drift + neutral return},\\
\text{restoring stasis} &= \text{zero equilibrium drift + attraction}.
\end{aligned}
}
\]

### Result 4. Oscillation forces feedback existence within the generalized model

For every candidate `phi<1`,

\[
G(\phi)=\frac{\phi^2-T\phi+D}{1-\phi}.
\]

If the local eigenvalues satisfy `0<=r_1,r_2<1`, taking `phi=r_1`, `alpha=r_2` gives `G=0`. Stable monotone return therefore does not establish feedback existence.

If the local eigenvalues are a non-real conjugate pair, the characteristic polynomial is positive for every real `phi`, so every admissible `phi<1` gives

\[
\boxed{G(\phi)>0.}
\]

Thus oscillatory local dynamics force feedback existence within this model class, although feedback magnitude remains unidentified.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 What changes relative to standard fluctuating-selection framing

The key distinction is not that short-term evolution can cancel over long times. It is that the amplitude and temporal fate of selection are constrained upstream by one ecological information structure. Finite sensing scope places ceilings on reward amplitude, and the same recurrent state space filters those rewards through community persistence.

### 4.2 Individual information use to evolutionary time

The causal hierarchy is

```text
individual information structure
  -> structural gap / productive obligations
  -> state-dependent selection or feedback gain
  -> community recurrence and endogenous response
  -> long-run fluctuation, stasis, or oscillatory restoration
```

This provides the individual-to-community-to-evolution connection without requiring a separate observation-design theory.

### 4.3 Productive frontier has a downstream ecological role

Productive frontier is retained only where it carries dynamical weight. A cap on the number of minimal productive obligations bounds the fixed cost and hence the adaptive gap. Theorem 2 then converts the required dynamical gap into a lower bound on `|H_min|`. Rank alone is not sufficient because sharp witnesses can have rank one.

### 4.4 Two kinds of long-term stasis

A small net long-term phenotype change does not identify one mechanism. Neutral cancellation preserves perturbations, whereas restoring feedback erases them. The distinction is dynamical, not semantic.

### 4.5 Why oscillation matters

Oscillation is not treated as an awkward special case. In the generalized local model it is the regime in which zero feedback becomes algebraically impossible. This gives oscillatory restoration a qualitatively different mechanistic status from monotone return.

### 4.6 Scope and limitations

Claims are restricted to local deterministic evolutionary coordinates, finite community-state dynamics, declared linear structural lifts, reversible chains for the sharp spectral ceiling, and finite deterministic unit-cost sensing for the information-complexity theorems. The paper does not provide stochastic finite-sample identification, observation design, multivariate quantitative genetics, mutation/migration/drift theory, or a global nonlinear bifurcation classification.
