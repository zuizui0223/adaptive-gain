# Villavicencio empirical positioning v1

Status: post-freeze empirical revision reserve. This file does **not** modify the frozen Evolution Letters V5 submission surface.

## Result to position

The annual Villavicencio fallback supports a narrow descriptive result:

> Under the same frozen conventional ecological filters, held-out annual link gains are substantially more discriminable than link losses, and this gain-over-loss asymmetry survives matching the two endpoint models on training prevalence and training information.

Phenological overlap is the only prespecified predictor block with reproducible incremental held-out information for both endpoints. In the matched-information audit, removing phenology reduces AUC for both gain and loss in every resample, but the reduction is consistently much larger for gain.

This is an **endpoint asymmetry in response discrimination**. It is not yet evidence that link assembly and disassembly are generated by different causal mechanisms.

## What is not novel

Do not claim novelty for the generic importance of phenology or abundance in plant-pollinator network dynamics.

Relevant prior art includes:

- Peralta et al. (2020, Ecology Letters, DOI 10.1111/ele.13510): in the same six-year Villavicencio system, phenological overlap and trait matching were associated with greater interaction stability, and phenological overlap was highlighted as useful for anticipating interaction change.
- CaraDonna et al. (2017, Ecology Letters, DOI 10.1111/ele.12740): phenology and relative abundance constrained within-season interaction turnover and rewiring.
- Domínguez-Garcia et al. (2026, Ecology Letters, DOI 10.1111/ele.70293): year-to-year rewiring among permanent species was associated with changes in phenology and abundance, especially of pollinators.

Therefore the empirical contribution cannot be framed as “phenology matters for rewiring.”

## Candidate incremental contribution

The defensible candidate is narrower:

1. define gain and loss as different risk sets rather than pooling all changed dyads;
2. use the same frozen ecological-filter surface for both endpoints;
3. hold out complete annual response transitions;
4. observe substantially stronger discrimination for gains than losses;
5. show that the asymmetry persists after forcing gain and loss training sets to contain the same number of positive and negative examples;
6. show that phenology contributes positive discrimination to both endpoints but substantially more to gains.

This asks a different question from whether rewiring exists or whether phenology constrains it:

> **Are the ecological filters that make a new interaction feasible equally informative about whether an existing interaction disappears?**

The current answer in Villavicencio is no at the descriptive prediction level: the measured conventional filters separate formation events much more strongly than loss events.

## Why this could matter biologically

A large gain-loss discrimination asymmetry is compatible with interaction formation being strongly filtered by ecological opportunity, whereas persistence or disappearance may depend more heavily on processes not captured by the current filter set, such as encounter stochasticity, abundance fluctuations at finer temporal grain, competition, behavioural history, demographic change, or imperfect detection.

Those mechanisms are **hypotheses**, not results. The present analysis establishes only that the measured information surface is much more diagnostic for gains.

This distinction is potentially useful because many network-turnover analyses combine link additions and losses into one turnover or rewiring magnitude. If formation and loss respond differently to the same ecological filters, pooling directions can hide biologically meaningful structure.

## Time-support limitation

The annual response holdout is not a strict forecasting design.

In the public workbook:

- phenological overlap is a study-wide six-year pairwise measure;
- flower abundance is an overall six-year plant measure;
- morphology is species-level.

The held-out object is the annual link response, not the time support used to construct every predictor.

Use:
- “held-out annual-link discrimination”;
- “retrospective predictive-filter association”;
- “gain-loss predictive asymmetry.”

Do not use:
- “future-link forecast”;
- “out-of-time prediction from past-only covariates”;
- “prospective temporal validation.”

## Post-hoc status

The gain-over-loss directional pattern was noticed before the matched-information audit was designed. Therefore:

- the original full-model gain/loss difference is descriptive;
- the matched-information analysis is a retrospective robustness check;
- the 100-resample sign consistency is not a preregistered significance test;
- empirical resampling quantiles are not confirmatory confidence intervals.

## What would promote this to a standalone ecological result

A confirmatory follow-up should freeze the gain-versus-loss asymmetry before analysis and use predictors with appropriate temporal support.

Preferred design:

1. reconstruct year- or subseason-specific phenology and abundance when possible;
2. define separate gain and loss risk sets prospectively;
3. freeze the common predictor set before endpoint comparison;
4. use response transitions never used to construct time-varying predictors;
5. compare discrimination with prevalence-insensitive metrics and calibrated proper scores;
6. repeat across independent sites or network systems;
7. explicitly audit detection / sampling effort;
8. treat mechanistic explanations for the asymmetry as secondary unless independently identified.

A replicated result would support the ecological proposition that interaction assembly and disappearance expose different amounts of structure to conventional ecological filters.

## Relation to routeability

Do not use this asymmetry as routeability evidence.

The Villavicencio analysis contains no independently measured decision-equivalence classes, cue hierarchy, contingent-routing cost, or routeability contrast. Its role is to establish a concrete ecological response surface and a directional empirical pattern that a later decision-structure layer could attempt to explain.

The hierarchy remains:

`exact routeability theory -> natural-network response/filter asymmetry -> independent decision layer -> direct routeability experiment`

The second arrow is empirical; the third remains unfilled in the observational data.
