# Novelty paragraph v3

Evolutionary ecology already treats information acquisition, sampling, memory, and information-processing limits as factors shaping adaptive decisions and evolutionary responses. Computer science and test theory separately characterize fixed separating systems, adaptive decision trees, multiway tests, and adaptivity gaps, while fluctuating-selection and eco-evolutionary theory already explain how rapid short-term change can fail to accumulate or can be dynamically restored. We therefore do not claim novelty for information use, cognitive constraints, sequential sampling, bounded-outcome decision trees, the bounded-arity tree extremal formulas used internally, adaptivity, rapid evolution, stasis, or feedback themselves. Our contribution lies in their ecological composition. We show that finite sensing complexity places exact ceilings on structurally generated selection, that those ceilings combine with recurrence of the same community states to bound long-run evolutionary fluctuation, and that a required feedback regime implies a minimum or Pareto-minimal finite information structure. Thus the finite combinatorial structure of individual information use restricts which evolutionary timescales and local feedback phases are reachable.
