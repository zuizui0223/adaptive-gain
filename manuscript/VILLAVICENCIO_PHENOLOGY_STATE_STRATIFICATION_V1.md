# Villavicencio phenology state stratification v1

Status: post-result ecological diagnostic for PR #65. This file does **not** modify the frozen Evolution Letters V5 submission surface.

## Main pattern

The annual fallback does not contain a year-specific phenology exposure. It contains one dyad-level phenological-overlap matrix that is reused across annual transitions. That makes it inappropriate for testing whether phenological **shifts** cause rewiring, but it is well suited to asking how baseline temporal overlap stratifies dyad states.

In the sampling-consistent 2008-2011 primary scope, pooled median overlap is:

- stable absent: 0
- gain: 12
- loss: 13
- retained: 20

The same ordering is visually stable in all three held-out transitions: stable absences remain at median 0, gains and losses occupy an intermediate-overlap region, and retained links have the highest overlap.

## Three contrasts

Using phenological overlap alone as a ranking variable:

1. **gain vs stable absence:** AUC = 0.9055 pooled; 0.9028, 0.9036 and 0.9128 across the three primary transitions.
2. **retained vs loss:** AUC = 0.6613 pooled; 0.6718, 0.6611 and 0.6492 across transitions.
3. **gain vs loss among changed dyads:** AUC = 0.4907 pooled; 0.4466, 0.4970 and 0.5111 across transitions.

The third contrast is the key interpretation check. Phenological overlap is highly informative about whether a previously absent dyad is a plausible candidate for formation, and moderately informative about persistence of an existing link, but it contains essentially no information about whether a changed link is a gain or a loss.

## Ecological interpretation

The cleanest interpretation is therefore not “phenology drives rewiring direction.”

It is:

> **Phenological overlap behaves as an interaction eligibility and persistence filter: low-overlap dyads are overwhelmingly stably absent, high-overlap realized links are more likely to persist, and changing links occupy an intermediate overlap region whose direction is not resolved by phenology alone.**

This gives a concrete meaning to the phrase *predictive filter*. Phenology narrows the feasible interaction surface. It does not specify the remaining directional transition.

This also explains why gain discrimination is stronger than loss discrimination without requiring a claim that assembly and disassembly are generated by different causal mechanisms. The gain risk set contains many structurally implausible low-overlap dyads, so phenology removes a large fraction of the candidate surface. Existing links have already passed that filter, leaving a narrower overlap range in which other processes can dominate persistence versus loss.

## Relation to Peralta et al. (2020)

The source paper already showed that phenologically well-matched interactions form a more stable backbone and explicitly proposed phenological overlap as useful for predicting changes in interactions. This diagnostic should therefore not be sold as discovery of a new role for phenology.

Its additional value is the directional state decomposition:

- stable absence;
- gain;
- loss;
- retention.

That decomposition shows that the strong phenology signal is concentrated in **eligibility/retention**, while gain versus loss among already-changing links is effectively unresolved.

## Link to the routeability program

This result improves the empirical bridge without validating routeability.

It identifies what the conventional ecological layer appears to do:

**phenology -> feasible/persistent interaction set**

and, just as importantly, what it does not do:

**phenology -/-> direction of change among changing links**

That residual directional problem is where an independently measured decision layer could add information. The routeability test must still be supplied independently rather than inferred from these network states.

## Claim ceiling

Allowed:

- baseline phenological overlap strongly filters candidate link formation;
- higher overlap is associated with retention among established links;
- gains and losses have nearly indistinguishable overlap distributions in this annual fallback;
- the observed gain-discrimination advantage is parsimoniously compatible with pre-filtering of candidate links.

Not allowed:

- annual phenological change caused rewiring;
- phenology determines gain versus loss;
- assembly and disassembly are proven to be distinct causal processes;
- the intermediate-overlap region is a universal threshold or “turnover zone”;
- this result validates routeability.
