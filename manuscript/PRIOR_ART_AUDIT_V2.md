# Prior-art audit v2 — narrowed boundary

## Purpose

This note updates the remaining novelty audit after targeted searches in evolutionary ecology, test cover, separating systems, and decision-tree theory. It is deliberately conservative.

## 1. Sequential and adaptive information use is established prior art

Evolutionary ecology already treats information acquisition and use as adaptive decision making, including repeated sampling, state dependence, and ecological consequences. Dall et al. (2005) and Schmidt et al. (2010) are sufficient anchor references for this boundary.

Therefore the manuscript must not claim novelty for:

- sequential cue use;
- adaptive information acquisition;
- cue ordering;
- state-dependent information use;
- feedback between action and subsequently available information.

The manuscript contribution begins only after a finite sensing task has been mapped to a structural constraint on downstream evolutionary dynamics.

## 2. Fixed separation and adaptive decision trees are established prior art

The fixed identification problem overlaps classical separating systems / minimum test set / test cover. The adaptive problem overlaps optimal decision trees and adaptive search. Multiway decision trees are also established; e.g. Chakaravarthy et al. (ICALP 2009) explicitly study decision trees with multiway branches, and later optimal-decision-tree work treats tests with multiple outcomes.

Therefore the manuscript must not claim novelty for:

- binary or multiway decision trees;
- bounded numbers of outcomes per test as a modelling idea;
- minimum separating systems;
- fixed-vs-adaptive cost comparisons;
- adaptivity gaps.

The candidate contribution remains the ecological composition:

`required dynamical regime -> required structural gap -> minimum/Pareto-minimal finite information structure`.

## 3. Important distinction: bounded test size is not bounded query arity

There is a substantial classical literature on separating systems / Test Cover with bounded test size, beginning with Katona (1966), later Wegener (1979), and modern parameterized work such as Crowston et al. (Algorithmica 2016, DOI 10.1007/s00453-014-9948-7).

Those papers constrain the cardinality of a test set (how many items lie in one separating subset). That is not the same object as the repository's bounded query arity `b`, which constrains the number of outcomes/parts generated by one query.

Do not cite bounded-test-size results as if they proved the repository's bounded-outcome extremal formulas. They are useful neighboring prior art, not an equivalence.

Multiway decision-tree literature is the more relevant conceptual prior art for bounded outcome arity.

## 4. Productive frontier: no exact published synonym identified yet

The audit found standard language for minimal / irredundant test covers and separating systems, but no source yet establishing an exact equivalence to the repository's productive frontier: the family of minimal mandatory separation obligations that determines the fixed-side cost after quotienting redundant structure.

This does not establish novelty. Until a deeper combinatorial audit is complete, use the cautious formulation:

- `productive frontier` is repository terminology;
- its role is to retain only the irreducible separation obligations relevant to the declared fixed-cost problem;
- edge-count consequences should be stated as consequences of the declared construction rather than as a newly named classical invariant.

## 5. Ecology-side constraints on information processing are also prior art

The targeted ecology search found several closer predecessors than generic information-use reviews:

- Dukas (2004) treats limited attention / restricted information-processing rate as an evolutionary-ecological constraint with fitness consequences.
- Work on complex cues and plasticity argues that richer cue integration can increase adaptive plasticity and reduce mismatches.
- Wright (2022) explicitly models sampling effort, memory, cue reliability, temporal autocorrelation, and plasticity jointly; sampling costs limit the adaptive upper range of plastic responses and memory becomes advantageous when temporal autocorrelation is high.

Therefore the manuscript must not claim novelty for the broad idea that limited information processing, sampling costs, cue reliability, or memory can constrain evolutionary responses.

The remaining candidate distinction is much narrower and more formal:

`finite discrete decision/separation complexity -> exact structural ceiling -> exact reachability bound on long-run fluctuation or local feedback phase`.

The audit has not identified a direct ecological predecessor with this full exact composition. This is evidence for a candidate distinction, not proof of first-ever novelty.

## 6. Shared-state-space reward and recurrence

Markov-switching environments, fluctuating selection, temporal autocorrelation, and eco-evolutionary feedback are all established. The remaining candidate distinction is narrower: the same finite community-state space both

1. carries structurally generated rewards `g_i`, and
2. supplies the transition operator that determines temporal recurrence,

with an exact structural-temporal ceiling derived from this composition.

The audit has not yet identified a prior ecological model using this exact finite-information-to-reward construction and then deriving the same sharp joint ceiling. Again, phrase as a conservative composition result, not a priority claim.

## 7. Updated novelty boundary

The strongest defensible statement is now:

> Information use, sequential decision making, cognitive/information-processing constraints, separating systems, multiway decision trees, adaptivity gaps, fluctuating selection, and eco-evolutionary feedback are all prior art. The contribution of the present framework is the exact composition of finite information-structure bounds with evolutionary dynamics: structurally generated selection is bounded by finite sensing complexity, community recurrence filters those same state-indexed rewards through time, and required dynamical regimes imply minimum or Pareto-minimal finite information structures. The novelty claim therefore concerns ecological reachability under declared finite information constraints, not the underlying decision-tree, test-cover, cognitive-constraint, or dynamical-systems machinery.

## 8. Remaining checks before submission

Only two targeted checks remain genuinely material:

1. whether the exact repository bounded-outcome extremal recurrence/formulas have a published equivalent in multiway identification/test theory;
2. whether an ecological/evolutionary paper already derives a comparable exact reachability ceiling from finite cue/decision complexity into evolutionary feedback phases or long-run fluctuation.

The productive-frontier naming question is now secondary: attribution can be handled conservatively even if no exact synonym is found.
