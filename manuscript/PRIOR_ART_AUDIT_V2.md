# Prior-art audit v2 — narrowed boundary

## Purpose

This note updates the remaining novelty audit after targeted searches in evolutionary ecology, test cover, separating systems, and decision-tree theory. It is deliberately conservative.

## 1. Sequential and adaptive information use is established prior art

Evolutionary ecology already treats information acquisition and use as adaptive decision making, including repeated sampling, state dependence, and ecological consequences. Dall et al. (2005) and Schmidt et al. (2010) are sufficient anchor references for this boundary.

Therefore the manuscript must not claim novelty for sequential cue use, adaptive information acquisition, cue ordering, state-dependent information use, or feedback between action and subsequently available information.

## 2. Fixed separation and adaptive decision trees are established prior art

The fixed identification problem overlaps classical separating systems / minimum test set / test cover. The adaptive problem overlaps optimal decision trees and adaptive search. Multiway decision trees are also established; Chakaravarthy et al. (ICALP 2009) explicitly study decision trees with multiway branches, and later work treats tests with multiple outcomes.

Therefore the manuscript must not claim novelty for binary or multiway decision trees, bounded numbers of outcomes per test as a modelling idea, minimum separating systems, fixed-vs-adaptive cost comparisons, or adaptivity gaps.

## 3. Bounded test size is not bounded query arity

Classical separating-system / Test Cover work by Katona, Wegener, Crowston and others constrains the cardinality of a test set. The repository parameter `b` instead constrains the number of outcomes/parts generated by one query. These are neighboring but different restrictions.

Do not cite bounded-test-size results as equivalence proofs for bounded outcome arity. Multiway decision-tree literature is the relevant conceptual prior art.

## 4. The bounded-arity extremal recurrence is not a manuscript novelty claim

The repository quantity `F_b(n,h)` is the maximum number of internal-node occurrences in a productive rooted decision tree with bounded leaf count, depth, and out-degree. Its recurrence is elementary rooted-tree decomposition, and the downstream sharp constructions use standard decision-tree / separating-test ingredients.

A targeted search did not locate an exact published formula in identical notation, but this is no longer a material novelty question. The manuscript should explicitly treat the recurrence, endpoint formulas, private-pair witness construction, and bounded-arity extremal counts as imported, standard, or independently re-derived combinatorial machinery.

No priority claim should depend on whether an identical `F_b` formula appears in print.

## 5. Productive frontier: repository terminology, conservative attribution

The audit found standard language for minimal / irredundant test covers and separating systems, but no exact published synonym for the repository's productive frontier. This does not establish novelty.

Use the cautious formulation:

- `productive frontier` is repository terminology for the irreducible separation obligations retained by the declared fixed-cost problem;
- edge-count consequences are consequences of that construction;
- do not claim a new classical invariant.

## 6. Ecology-side information constraints are also prior art

Closer predecessors include:

- Dukas (2004): limited attention / restricted information-processing rate as an evolutionary-ecological constraint with fitness consequences;
- work on complex cues and plasticity: richer cue integration can increase adaptive plasticity and reduce phenotype-environment mismatch;
- Wright (2022): sampling effort, memory, cue reliability, temporal autocorrelation, and plasticity are jointly modelled; sampling costs limit plasticity and memory becomes advantageous under temporal autocorrelation.

Therefore the manuscript must not claim novelty for the broad idea that finite cognition, information-processing limits, sampling costs, memory, or cue reliability constrain evolutionary response.

The candidate distinction is narrower:

`finite discrete decision/separation complexity -> exact structural ceiling -> exact reachability bound on long-run evolutionary fluctuation or local feedback phase`.

## 7. Shared-state-space reward and recurrence

Markov-switching environments, fluctuating selection, temporal autocorrelation, and eco-evolutionary feedback are all established. The candidate composition is that the same finite community-state space both carries structurally generated rewards `g_i` and supplies the transition operator determining temporal recurrence, allowing an exact structural-temporal ceiling.

The targeted audit has not identified a direct ecological predecessor with the full construction

`finite sensing complexity -> structural reward ceiling + state recurrence -> sharp long-run fluctuation ceiling / feedback-phase reachability`.

This is evidence for a defensible synthesis/reachability distinction, not proof of first-ever priority.

## 8. Updated novelty boundary

The strongest defensible statement is:

> Information use, sequential decision making, cognitive/information-processing constraints, separating systems, multiway decision trees, adaptivity gaps, fluctuating selection, and eco-evolutionary feedback are prior art. We also make no novelty claim for the bounded-arity tree extremal formulas used internally. The contribution of the present framework is their exact ecological composition: finite sensing complexity bounds structurally generated selection, community recurrence filters those same state-indexed rewards through time, and required dynamical regimes imply minimum or Pareto-minimal finite information structures. The novelty claim therefore concerns evolutionary reachability under declared finite information constraints, not the underlying cognitive, combinatorial, or dynamical machinery.

## 9. Only remaining material audit before submission

One targeted priority check remains materially relevant:

> Does an ecological/evolutionary paper already derive a comparable exact reachability theorem that maps finite cue/decision complexity into a ceiling on long-run evolutionary fluctuation or into minimum information complexity required for a feedback phase?

Even if no such predecessor is found, avoid `first-ever` language unless the final submission search is exhaustive enough to justify it.
