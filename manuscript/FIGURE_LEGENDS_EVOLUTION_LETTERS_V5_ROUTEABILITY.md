# Figure legends and alt text — environmental routeability

## Figure 1. Same ecological diversity can have different effective complexity

**A**, a routeable community contains eight resources whose identity can be narrowed by an early group cue and then by a branch-specific cue. Only the realized branch generates later information costs. **B**, a non-routeable community has the same resource richness, but many resource-specific distinctions remain jointly relevant. **C**, the resulting task-specific pair of contingent and precommitted resolution costs can differ strongly even when raw richness is identical. The ecological conclusion is that diversity does not determine effective complexity by itself; the cost of heterogeneity depends on whether ecological alternatives can be partitioned into conditionally relevant branches. The pair (C_A,C_F) is task-specific and is not a universal scalar score of community complexity.

**Alt text:** Three-panel schematic comparing communities with the same eight resource types. Panel A shows a hierarchical routeable structure in which an early group cue directs the consumer to only one branch-specific later cue. Panel B shows the same resource richness without branch-specific routing, so many terminal distinctions remain jointly relevant. Panel C compares contingent and precommitted resolution costs and states that diversity is not equivalent to effective complexity.

## Figure 2. Raw richness and interaction-relevant richness can diverge

**A**, adding taxonomically distinct ecological states inside an existing decision-equivalence class increases raw richness without changing the focal decision obligation. **B**, adding a single state that creates a new branch-specific distinction increases interaction-relevant richness even though raw richness changes only slightly. **C**, routeable generalism can place many resources into a small number of decision classes per encounter, whereas non-routeable generalism retains more independent terminal distinctions. Routeability therefore modifies the information cost of broad niche use; it does not determine niche breadth by itself.

**Alt text:** Three-panel schematic separating raw richness from decision-relevant richness. Panel A shows several species collapsing into one decision-equivalence class, so species richness increases while focal decision complexity remains unchanged. Panel B shows one additional ecological state creating a new decision branch. Panel C contrasts routeable and non-routeable broad resource use, emphasizing that routeability changes information burden but does not by itself cause generalism.

## Figure 3. Routeability filters realized interactions and temporal ecological effects

**A**, the same consumer can have a potential interaction network containing several resources on the basis of encounter, morphology and energetic compatibility. **B**, environmental routeability can change the subset of those links that is behaviorally accessible within a fixed ecological budget. More potential links remain accessible in the routeable case; in the non-routeable case, unresolved information costs exclude some links. Behaviorally accessible interactions remain only a subset of the determinants of realized interactions because competition, demography, morphology and spatial co-occurrence can remove additional links. **C**, two environmental sequences can have the same physical persistence but different long-run consequences if one recurrent mode contrasts states with different decision-relevant structure while the other contrasts decision-equivalent states. Environmental autocorrelation therefore matters through persistence of interaction-relevant heterogeneity, not autocorrelation alone.

**Alt text:** Three-panel schematic linking routeability to community and temporal ecology. Panel A shows one consumer with the same dashed potential links to several resources. Panel B compares a routeable environment, where more links are behaviorally accessible within the same budget, with a non-routeable environment, where fewer links can be resolved. Panel C compares two recurrent environmental sequences with equal persistence but different decision relevance, showing that only decision-relevant recurrence strongly contributes through this mechanism.

## Figure interpretation boundaries

- Routeability is task-specific and relational, not a universal scalar property of a community.
- Same richness does not imply same effective complexity.
- Decision-equivalence is defined only for the declared focal ecological action.
- Routeability can modify the information cost of broad niche use; it does not imply routeability alone causes generalism.
- Behaviorally accessible interactions are not automatically realized interactions.
- Environmental persistence matters through alignment with interaction-relevant structure, not autocorrelation alone.
- Natural-history examples remain interpretations of the exact structural results rather than fitted empirical demonstrations.
