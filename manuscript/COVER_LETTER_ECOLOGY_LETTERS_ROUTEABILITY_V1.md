# Cover letter — Ecology Letters routeability v2

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Conditional decision topology separates ecological diversity from decision complexity,”** as a Letter in *Ecology
Letters*.

A long-standing problem in behavioural ecology is why some generalists can
choose efficiently among many resources whereas other broad choice sets impose
large information-processing costs. The neural-constraints literature treats
breadth as potentially costly, while decision ecology and sequential-cue
models emphasize that natural choices are often hierarchical: animals need
not compare every resource or cue simultaneously. What has been missing is an
exact answer to the question that connects these views: **when does ecological
diversity actually become decision complexity?**

We formalize that question by separating the cost of a fixed resolving cue set
((C_F)) from the minimum worst-case cost when later cues may depend on
earlier outcomes ((C_A)). For deterministic exact resolution, the complete
static subset-information function determines (C_F). Yet we construct two
12-state ecological decision tasks on one identical physical cue matrix with
the same complete Shannon entropy vector and the same (C_F=4), but different
adaptive costs, (C_A=4) versus 3. Cartesian products preserve complete
entropic equivalence while making this adaptive-cost difference arbitrarily
large. A separate exactly balanced family gives an unbounded
fixed-to-adaptive ratio under weaker information matching.

The ecological implication is not that sequential choice is new. Rather,
resource richness, cue richness and even complete static Shannon information
need not identify the processing burden faced by an animal that can use early
observations to determine what must be inspected next. A finite natural-history
budget makes this distinction operational when (C_A\le B<C_F): contingent
sensing can then guarantee a resolved action when fixed acquisition cannot.

We ground the theory in established animal behaviour without treating any
existing dataset as a direct theorem test. Bumblebees provide a model flexible
generalist: experiments show selective recruitment of secondary cues,
context-dependent cue relevance, sequential sensory sampling and switching
between cue-regulated foraging routines. The same staged structure occurs in
parasitoid host finding, multi-stage predator avoidance in fiddler crabs and
patch foraging across animals. These systems motivate the theory's scope; they
do not estimate (C_A) or (C_F).

A secondary result shows how the structural distinction can propagate beyond
immediate behaviour. For any nondecreasing Lipschitz sensing-to-selection
lift, insufficient between-state routeability contrast imposes a necessary
no-go on a specified local eco-evolutionary feedback regime. We deliberately
keep this result subordinate to the paper's primary ecological contribution:
a theory of effective decision complexity.

We believe the manuscript fits *Ecology Letters* because it connects a
long-standing behavioural-ecology problem to an exact mathematical result,
defines a measurable distinction between nominal ecological diversity and
effective decision burden, and generates direct experiments for natural
decision systems.

We explicitly do not claim novelty for sequential decision making, decision
trees, all-subset entropy profiles, partial information decomposition, active
feature acquisition or the generic fact that entropy can miss operational
structure. The manuscript-specific contribution is the exact separation
between static ecological information and adaptive resolution cost, together
with its finite-budget ecological consequence.

The manuscript is within the journal's Letter limits: the abstract is 150
words, the main text is approximately 4,421 words by the repository submission
counter, and there are two main figures.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
