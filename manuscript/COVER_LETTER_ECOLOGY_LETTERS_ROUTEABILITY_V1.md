# Cover letter — Ecology Letters routeability v3

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Conditional decision topology separates
ecological diversity from decision complexity,”** as a Letter in *Ecology
Letters*.

A long-standing problem in behavioural ecology is why some broad choice sets
impose large information-processing costs whereas others can be handled
efficiently by sequential decisions. The neural-constraints literature treats
breadth as potentially costly, while decision ecology and sequential-cue
models emphasize that natural choices are often hierarchical: animals need not
compare every resource or cue simultaneously. What has been missing is an
exact answer to the question connecting these views: **when does ecological
diversity actually become decision complexity?**

We formalize that question by separating the cost of a fixed resolving cue set
(\(C_F\)) from the minimum worst-case cost when later cues may depend on
earlier outcomes (\(C_A\)). For deterministic exact resolution, the complete
static subset-information function determines \(C_F\). Yet we construct two
12-state ecological decision tasks on one identical physical cue matrix with
the same complete Shannon entropy vector and the same \(C_F=4\), but different
adaptive costs, \(C_A=4\) versus 3. Cartesian products preserve complete
entropic equivalence while making this adaptive-cost difference arbitrarily
large. A separate exactly balanced family gives an unbounded fixed-to-adaptive
ratio under weaker information matching.

The ecological implication is not that sequential choice is new. Rather,
resource richness, cue richness and even complete static Shannon information
need not identify the processing burden faced by an animal that can use early
observations to determine what must be inspected next. A finite natural-history
budget makes this distinction operational when \(C_A\le B<C_F\): contingent
sensing can then guarantee a resolved action when fixed acquisition cannot.
Exact target-relevant reductions also show when nominally distinct states or
cues are decision-redundant for a focal action.

A minimal temporal extension supplies a directly behavioural consequence. An
early cue can have zero direct information about the final target yet remain
valuable because it predicts which later cue will be useful. In the symmetric
two-step model the contingent accuracy advantage is
\(G_{\rm time}=|2\rho-1|/4\), and therefore disappears exactly at temporal
independence. With symmetric cue noise, temporal predictability, routing-cue
reliability and specialist-cue reliability enter as multiplicative gates.

We ground the theory in established animal behaviour without treating any
existing dataset as a direct theorem test. Bumblebee flower choice, parasitoid
host finding, multi-stage predator avoidance in fiddler crabs and patch
foraging provide examples of staged or conditional information use in
different ecological settings.

We believe the manuscript fits *Ecology Letters* because it connects a
long-standing behavioural-ecology problem to exact mathematical results,
defines a measurable distinction between nominal ecological diversity and
effective decision burden, and produces direct predictions about when
context-dependent information acquisition should matter.

We explicitly do not claim novelty for sequential decision making, decision
trees, all-subset entropy profiles, partial information decomposition, active
feature acquisition, temporal autocorrelation or generic value of information.
The manuscript-specific contribution is the exact separation between static
ecological information and adaptive resolution cost, together with its
finite-budget and predictive-routing consequences.

The manuscript is within the journal's Letter limits: the abstract is 150
words, the main text is approximately 4,426 words by the repository submission
counter, and there are two main figures.
